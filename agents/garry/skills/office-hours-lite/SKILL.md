# Garry Office Hours Lite

This skill borrows the useful parts of gstack `/office-hours` without importing the full workflow.

Use it before `decision-pipeline` when an idea is still fuzzy, emotionally exciting, or not yet supported by evidence.

## Core Rule

Do not accept the founder's framing too quickly.

Find the customer, the urgent pain, the status quo, the narrow wedge, and the riskiest assumption before recommending code.

## Workflow

1. Restate the idea in one sentence.
2. Choose the right stage:
   - `Pre-product`
   - `Has users`
   - `Has paying customers`
   - `Internal/tooling`
   - `Side project/learning`
3. Ask only the questions needed for that stage.
4. Push until answers are specific, behavioral, and evidence-based.
5. Give a verdict:
   - `Build now`
   - `Prototype first`
   - `Research first`
   - `Park`
   - `Kill`
6. If worth building, route into `decision-pipeline`.

## Forcing Questions

Use these selectively. Do not ask all six by default.

### 1. Demand Reality

What is the strongest evidence that someone actually wants this?

Look for behavior, not compliments:

- paid
- manually hacked a workaround
- asked repeatedly
- switched from another tool
- uses a bad workaround because the pain is real

### 2. Status Quo

What do they do today instead?

If there is no current workaround, the pain may not be urgent.

### 3. Desperate Specificity

Who is the narrowest real person or group with this problem right now?

Avoid vague users like "creators," "founders," "busy people," or "small businesses" unless Annabel can name the exact segment and moment of pain.

### 4. Narrowest Wedge

What is the smallest version that would make one specific user say, "I need this"?

Prefer a sharp wedge over a broad platform.

### 5. Observation

Has Annabel watched the problem happen?

If not, recommend a customer conversation, manual workflow, or direct observation before productizing.

### 6. Future Fit

Why now, and why does this get more important over time?

Avoid trend-chasing unless the trend creates a concrete new behavior or buyer urgency.

## Output

Return:

- Blunt take
- Customer and pain
- Evidence level
- Riskiest assumption
- Smallest real-world test
- Verdict
- Next move

## Stop Conditions

Stop and ask one pointed question if:

- the user/buyer is unclear
- the pain is abstract
- there is no current workaround
- the idea is really three ideas bundled together
- the next step depends on missing evidence
