# Annabel Personal AI Site Plan

## Core Positioning

Annabel is an AI tinkerer, builder, and small-business systems partner.

The site should not say, "AI will replace people." It should say:

> I help business owners work on their business, not only in their business.

The emotional promise:

> AI should give people time back for the parts of the work they actually like: creativity, strategy, customers, relationships, community, and the reason they started the business in the first place.

The professional promise:

> I help small businesses use AI, website improvements, and lightweight automation to make their marketing, operations, and customer experience clearer and easier to run.

The personal hook:

> I am an AI tinkerer. I like playing with new tools, testing what is useful, and turning messy workflows into simple systems that real people can actually use.

## Site Personality

The site should feel like Annabel:

- Curious and systems-minded
- Warm, but not casual to the point of feeling unprofessional
- Design-sensitive, not generic AI/SaaS
- Human-first about AI
- Forward-thinking without pretending to have every answer
- Builder energy: visible projects, experiments, and proof of work
- Grounded in small business reality, not abstract tech hype

Avoid:

- Fear-based AI messaging
- "AI will replace your team" language
- Generic consultant speak
- Overly corporate agency design
- Too much technical jargon above the fold
- Cheesy floral graphics

## Visual Direction

Overall feel:

> Earthy, polished, botanical, personal, and smart.

Palette:

- Warm ivory: `#FAF7F1`
- Deep clay red: `#7E2F2F`
- Forest green: `#26483A`
- Muted olive: `#7A7F4B`
- Dusty rose: `#D8A29A`
- Warm charcoal: `#2D2926`

Design notes:

- Use Annabel's Michigan headshot prominently on the homepage.
- Include botanical/flowery details, but make them refined: pressed-flower linework, small floral dividers, subtle organic motifs, or illustrated accents.
- Keep plenty of white/ivory space so the site feels professional.
- Use earthy red and green as anchor colors, not loud blocks everywhere.
- The site should feel interesting to look at, but still easy to read and navigate.

Typography direction:

- Heading font: editorial serif such as Fraunces, Libre Baskerville, Cormorant Garamond, or a similar polished serif.
- Body font: clean sans-serif such as Inter, Lato, Source Sans, or similar.
- Tone: elegant but not precious.

## AI Automation Page

Purpose: show that Annabel is the AI person, and explain what that actually means for a small business.

Opening:

> AI is not supposed to take over the work that makes a business human. It should take pressure off the repetitive work so owners and teams can get back to strategy, creativity, relationships, and growth.

Where AI helps:

- Repetitive writing
- Outreach drafts
- Customer follow-up
- Newsletter/social repurposing
- Sponsor or partner research
- Admin workflows
- Internal SOPs
- Organizing messy notes, spreadsheets, docs, and emails
- Turning one idea into multiple useful marketing assets

Possible systems:

- Social/newsletter assistant
- Sponsor or partner outreach system
- Customer inquiry response helper
- Creator seeding or ambassador workflow
- Content repurposing workflow
- Internal AI workspace trained around business voice and examples

Marketing credibility section:

### Creator Marketing Example: Comfrt

Use Comfrt as an example of what small businesses can learn from modern creator-led commerce.

Careful framing:

> Comfrt is an example of how a simple product can become much bigger when the brand combines clear positioning, social proof, creator content, and a repeatable distribution system.

Reported examples to reference:

- Comfrt became known through TikTok and creator-led content around oversized/weighted comfort wear.
- Several marketing writeups and podcast descriptions point to micro-influencers, UGC, TikTok Shop, commissions, and an "always visible" content engine as key pieces of the brand's growth.
- One 2026 analysis reported that Comfrt used a very large affiliate network and high-volume creator content engine; treat exact numbers as third-party reporting, not verified company data.

Small business lesson:

- You do not need Comfrt's scale to borrow the method.
- The small-business version is: identify genuine fans, local creators, customers, partners, or community members; give them a simple content prompt; make posting easy; track what works; and use AI to help with outreach, follow-up, repurposing, and organization.

Engagement pod note:

- The method Annabel was remembering is called an engagement pod or influencer pod.
- Avoid recommending artificial engagement as the main strategy.
- Better framing: creator seeding, ambassador program, or community content sprint.
