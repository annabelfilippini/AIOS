# /sponsor-research

**When to use:** Before sending a first-touch email, when Bailey or Anya has a brand, category, city, or campus in mind and needs the AI to research fit, find contact paths, and propose the cleanest outreach angle.

**Owner:** Bailey

**How to use:** Paste the prompt below. This skill researches and structures the opportunity. It does *not* send outreach and does not invent facts.

---

## Prompt to paste

```
/sponsor-research

Research target:
[brand name, sponsor category, city, campus, event, or "open-ended"]

Mode:
- National / HQ partner
- City activation
- Campus launch
- Local sponsor
- Product seeding
- Collab

Context:
[what we already know. Examples: "launching 10 college campuses this fall," "Ann Arbor likely returning because Fleet Feet wants launch," "looking for smaller local brands that would show up in person," "hydration sponsor for Tuesday runs."]

Must include / avoid:
[optional. Examples: "avoid alcohol sponsors for campus launches," "include running stores and coffee shops," "do not use paid influencer databases."]

Research and return this exact structure:

1. FIT SUMMARY
   3 short bullets. Say why this target could make sense for cooldown specifically: 15+ chapters, weekly runs, college launches, apparel + run club, local chapter leaders, or the "1-5 miles at any pace + drinks after" ritual.

2. SOURCE NOTES
   5-8 bullets from public research. Include source labels or URLs when available. Mark anything uncertain as `[verify]`.

3. CONTACT PATHS
   - Best contact:
   - Backup contact:
   - Contact form / partnerships page:
   - LinkedIn / Instagram path:
   If you cannot find a real path, say exactly what is missing. Do not guess email addresses.

4. OUTREACH ANGLE
   One paragraph. Lead with the most real overlap, not generic "brand alignment." Name the specific ask that fits best: product seeding, chapter launch activation, table at a run, giveaway, 15-min intro, co-branded drop, or local intro.

5. FIT SCORE
   Score 1-5 with one sentence:
   - 5 = obvious fit, real audience overlap, contact path found, specific activation possible
   - 4 = strong fit, one missing detail
   - 3 = plausible, needs a sharper reason
   - 2 = weak, likely not worth first wave
   - 1 = avoid

6. RISK / WHY NOT
   2-4 bullets. Include category conflicts, bad audience fit, campus appropriateness, expensive activation needs, no public partnership signal, or anything that could make this feel forced.

7. READY-FOR-DRAFT INPUT
   Fill this out so Bailey can paste directly into `/sponsor-outreach`:

   Brand:
   Contact:
   What we want from them:
   Why they're a fit:
   Anchor:
   What I'd want to confirm before sending:

CRITICAL:
- Use public facts only. No invented contacts, campaigns, follower counts, sponsor history, or partnership history.
- If browsing is available, browse. If browsing is not available, say "research needed" and produce a research checklist instead of pretending.
- Sponsor outreach emails use sentence case, not lowercase.
- Keep the recommendation practical. cooldown needs outreach that Bailey can send, not a 20-page brand audit.
- Never auto-send anything.
```

---

## Notes

- Pair this with `/sponsor-outreach`. This skill decides whether the target is worth emailing and what the ask should be; `/sponsor-outreach` turns the chosen angle into Bailey's draft.
- For campus launches, prioritize sponsor categories that can physically show up or send product: running stores, recovery studios, coffee shops, smoothie/juice shops, fitness studios, wellness brands, local food brands, student-friendly national brands, and local apparel/printers.
- For national sponsors, look for public evidence of run-club, endurance, college, community, or event activations. If there is no public signal, the ask should be lighter: "15-min intro" or "would you be open to seeding one chapter?"

## What this saves

This turns the slowest part of Bailey's sponsor workflow — deciding if a brand is worth the email and finding the angle — into a repeatable 10-15 minute research pass. Estimated savings: **30-60 min per prospect**, plus fewer weak first-touch emails.
