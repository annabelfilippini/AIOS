# /launch-package

**When to use:** A new launch is going live — a product drop (apparel), a new chapter, a sponsor collab, a special event (like a Boulderthon activation). Replaces the "rebuild every marketing asset from scratch and forget the blog post" flow.

**Owner:** Both Bailey and Anya. Bailey for product/sponsor launches, Anya for chapter launches and ongoing content.

**How to use:** Paste the prompt below, fill in what's launching. The skill produces the full marketing kit — IG, email, homepage, blog — so nothing gets forgotten and everything sounds the same.

---

## Prompt to paste

```
/launch-package

What's launching (one line):
[e.g., "the Boulderthon Tank in lavender + navy," "the Cooldown Tampa chapter," "Tracksmith × Cooldown limited drop," "our 100th Tuesday run in Denver"]

Launch type (pick one):
- Product (apparel)
- Chapter (new city or college)
- Collab (co-branded with a partner)
- Event (one-time activation, race, anniversary)

When it goes live:
[date + time of day if it matters — e.g., "Friday May 9, 9am MT"]

What's special about it (3-5 bullets):
[the actual reason this matters. For a product: what it solves, what it pairs with, what makes it different from the existing line. For a chapter: who's leading it, what city ritual is being established, what's unique about that city. For a collab: what each side brings, what the artifact is, why this partner now. For an event: what's being celebrated, who's invited, what the moment is.]

Asset reference if available:
[e.g., "lifestyle photo at golden hour with chapter leader running," "studio shot front + back," "video of the chapter's first meetup" — describe what assets exist or "no assets yet, copy only"]

Sponsor / partner attribution (if any):
[e.g., "co-branded with Tracksmith, attribute @tracksmith," "sponsored by Strava, attribute on IG only" — leave blank if solo Cool Down]

Audience emphasis (pick one):
- Apparel buyers (the store side leans heavier)
- Run-club community (existing chapters and leaders lean heavier)
- Both equal

Anything to AVOID:
[optional — "don't mention price until launch day," "don't tag the partner publicly until 9am Friday," etc.]

Build the kit. Use this exact structure:

1. POSITIONING LINE
   The one sentence that should appear everywhere — homepage banner, IG bio link description, email subject, blog open. ≤15 words. Cool Down voice: lowercase, specific, no hype.

2. INSTAGRAM POST
   - Caption (≤40 words, lowercase, one exclamation max).
   - First-comment reply with the longer context (≤80 words — for people who tap "more").
   - 5-7 hashtag set in Cool Down style.

3. INSTAGRAM REEL
   - Three hook options (≤8 words each — the first line of the reel).
   - 4-beat structure for the reel itself: beat 1 (the moment), beat 2 (the detail), beat 3 (the shift), beat 4 (the invitation/CTA). Each beat ≤2 sentences of voiceover or overlay text.

4. INSTAGRAM STORY (3-frame sequence)
   - Frame 1: overlay text + image direction.
   - Frame 2: overlay text + image direction.
   - Frame 3: overlay text + the CTA (link sticker copy ≤6 words).

5. EMAIL SUBJECT LINES (5)
   Five options, ≤50 chars each. Mix: one specific (with a number or name), one curious, one direct, one playful, one quiet. Lowercase preferred but capital allowed if it earns it.

6. EMAIL BODY
   ≤150 words. In Cool Down voice. Opens with the moment or detail (not "we're so excited"). One CTA. Sign with the brand voice — Cool Down doesn't usually sign individual names on apparel/launch emails, but include if it fits.

7. HOMEPAGE BANNER COPY
   - Announcement bar text (≤60 chars).
   - Hero subhead (≤15 words) — the version that complements the existing "uniting the world through running" hero, not replaces it.

8. BLOG POST (300-500 words)
   The longer-form anchor that everything else links to. In Cool Down voice. Structure:
   - Open with the moment or the detail (the same one from the IG caption — this is the ONE story everything tells).
   - The middle: who/what/why (2-3 short paragraphs).
   - The close: the invitation — why this is for the reader and what to do next.
   Headline ≤8 words, lowercase.

9. RISKS / WHAT'S MISSING
   - Anything claimed in the inputs that needs verification before going live (numbers, partner attributions, specific names).
   - Assets that need to exist before this can ship (photo, video, partner logo, leader headshot).
   - Dates/timing that need confirming.

CRITICAL — read before drafting:
- Lowercase by default. Headers, taglines, CTAs lowercase. Body sentence case.
- One exclamation point across the ENTIRE kit, max. Choose the surface where it earns it.
- No "obsessed," "literally," "queen," "babe," "level up," "unlock," "iconic," "elite."
- Numbers over adjectives. "100th Tuesday run" beats "milestone moment."
- Pair contrast as a structural move where it fits ("you bring X. we bring Y." — adapt to the launch).
- Every surface tells the SAME story — the IG caption, email, blog, and banner should all reference the same moment, not three different angles.
- Never invent partner quotes, athlete endorsements, or specifics not in the input.

After the kit, give me ONE line:
- "The single strongest line in this kit:" — your pick of the line that should anchor the launch (1 sentence + which surface it came from).
```

---

## Notes on running this

- **Run this 3-5 days before launch, not the morning of.** Gives time to gather assets, fact-check the partner attribution, and edit copy.
- **The "single strongest line" output is the anchor.** When the launch goes live, that line should appear everywhere — IG caption, email subject, blog headline, homepage banner. Repetition across surfaces makes the launch feel coordinated.
- **For chapter launches**: include the chapter leader's name in the inputs. Chapters lead with the human, not the brand.
- **For product launches**: include the product name + price in the inputs. The skill won't invent a price, but it'll work the name in correctly.
- **For collab launches**: be specific about the partner attribution. "Tracksmith × Cooldown" vs "Cooldown × Tracksmith" matters in some partner contracts.
- **The blog post is load-bearing for SEO.** Cool Down has 4 staged blog section headers ("Movement Together," "Cooling Down with Cooldown," "Desk Job Wellness," "Cooldown with an Evening Ritual") and zero published posts. Every launch should produce one blog post — that's how the empty blog gets filled without a separate effort.

## What this saves

Per the audit: marketing for product launches and chapter launches is currently rebuilt-from-scratch each time, with the blog never getting written. This skill produces a coordinated kit in 5 min instead of 4-6 hrs of fragmented copywriting. Estimated savings: **3-5 hrs per launch, plus the blog gets written instead of skipped.**

Reliability is HIGH because every output is a draft Bailey/Anya approve before publishing. The risk is voice drift — the skill should be re-read and tightened (paste back: "Reread voice.md. Fix any line that breaks rule [#].") if a launch sounds off.
