# /chapter-caption

**When to use:** After a chapter leader sends a photo + a few words from this week's run. Replaces the "stare at blank caption box for 20 minutes per city" flow.

**Owner:** Anya

**How to use:** Paste the prompt below with the photo description and 2-sentence context from the leader. The more concrete the leader's notes (a real moment, a number, a weather detail), the sharper the captions.

---

## Prompt to paste

```
/chapter-caption

City:
[e.g., "Denver," "University of Michigan"]

What's in the photo:
[1-2 sentences describing the photo — group shot at the trailhead, post-run drinks at the bar, sunrise stretch circle, etc. If you have the photo attached, mention what's visible: how many people, where, what time of day, anything that stands out.]

Context from the leader (or what you know):
[2-3 sentences — how many showed up, the route, the weather, anything that happened, who was new, any milestone. Lift directly from the leader's text if they sent one.]

Recurring elements to mention (optional):
[any sponsor product on display, a recurring landmark, a milestone like "10th week running," a chapter-specific in-joke. Leave blank if none.]

Anything to AVOID:
[optional — "don't mention the rain," "don't tag the bar," etc.]

Draft the social. Use this exact structure:

1. CAPTION — VARIANT A (the warm-and-specific one)
   1-2 sentences, ≤30 words. Lead with a concrete detail (city + count + a real moment). End with one small invitation or sign-off, no exclamation. Cool Down voice: lowercase, specific, restrained.

2. CAPTION — VARIANT B (the energy-and-community one)
   1-2 sentences, ≤30 words. Slightly more energy than A — one exclamation point allowed if it earns it. Pair-contrast structure preferred ("X miles. Y people. one Z.").

3. CAPTION — VARIANT C (the one that drives a reply)
   Ends in a question or a direct invitation that's easy to answer. ≤30 words. Examples: "what was your favorite mile?" / "drop the gas-station coffee you're starting your week with." / "next tuesday, same place — coming?"

4. REEL HOOKS (3)
   Three short opening lines for a reel, ≤8 words each. These are spoken or overlay text, not captions. Punchy. No questions in the hook.
   Example shape: "tuesday night denver." / "fourteen of us. three miles."

5. STORY FRAME (1)
   Single line of overlay text for an IG story. ≤6 words. Lowercase. The image is doing the work — the text is the punctuation.

6. HASHTAG SET (5-7)
   Light hashtag set, Cool Down style. Include: city tag (#denvercooldown or equivalent), brand tag (#cooldownrunning), 2-3 generic running tags max, 1-2 city-specific running tags. No "fitfam," "runningmotivation," "runninglife," or other generic spam.

CRITICAL — read before drafting:
- Lowercase by default. Headers/CTAs lowercase. Body sentence case.
- One exclamation per caption, max. Stories and hooks should not need any.
- No emoji stacks. Max one emoji per caption — usually none. The Cool Down 🤍 is the most common when one is used.
- No "obsessed," "literally," "queen," "babe," "y'all," "fitfam," "runninglife."
- Numbers over adjectives. "14 of us" beats "great turnout."
- If the leader's context didn't give you a specific moment to anchor on, flag it — "input was generic; recommend asking the leader for one detail before posting."

After the drafts, give me TWO lines:
- "Strongest variant for THIS post:" — your honest pick of A/B/C and why (1 sentence).
- "What I'd ask the leader for next week:" — one concrete detail that would make next week's caption sharper.
```

---

## Notes on running this

- **The leader's context is the load-bearing input.** Generic context = generic captions. If the leader sent "great run today!", paste back: *"Anya — this is too generic to caption sharp. I drafted three variants but they're going to read like every other run-club post. Worth asking the leader for one specific detail (a count, a moment, a weather note, a person who showed up new) before posting."*
- **Run for one city at a time.** Don't batch 15 cities into one prompt. The output gets blurry.
- **Boulderthon / sponsor moments:** if the run had a sponsor presence (product on display, partner showing up), include in the "recurring elements" field. The skill will work them in naturally without making it feel like an ad.
- **For new chapters or college chapters** (e.g., University of Michigan): include in context. The voice should still be Cool Down voice, but the rituals and references differ from established city chapters.

## What this saves

Per the audit + research hypotheses: Anya spends an est. 8-15 hrs/week on social across 15+ chapters. This skill targets the caption-writing phase — roughly 30 min per chapter post → 2-3 min. Estimated savings: **5-8 hrs/week.** Reliability is HIGH because Anya still posts manually — the skill never touches the live account.
