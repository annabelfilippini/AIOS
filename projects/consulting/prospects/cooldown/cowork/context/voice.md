## Register — varies by surface

**Capitalization rules differ by where the copy is going.** This is the most important register rule and Cowork must check the surface before drafting.

| Surface | Capitalization | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Website (headers, CTAs, taglines, nav) | **lowercase** | brand-forward, deliberate, the visual signature |
| IG captions, stories, social copy | **lowercase** | matches website voice |
| Product descriptions, marketing copy | **lowercase** | brand-facing |
| **Sponsor outreach emails** | **standard sentence case (capitalize "I", proper nouns, sentence starts)** | emails are 1:1 communication — formality signals respect, lowercase reads sloppy to a partnerships contact |
| **Chapter launch invites to recurring sponsors** | **standard sentence case** | same — these are real people we want to show up |
| Newsletter copy | **lowercase** for headers/section labels, **sentence case** for body | mixed — body is read like an email, headers are brand surfaces |
| Customer service email replies | **standard sentence case** | 1:1, formal-ish, respect the customer |

**Rule of thumb:** if it's *brand → audience at scale* (site, social, product), lowercase. If it's *human → human directly* (email, DM to a sponsor, customer reply), sentence case.

The rest of the voice rules below apply to ALL surfaces — only capitalization changes.

- **Warm energy without hype.** They sound excited *about people*, not about products. Never "OBSESSED 💯" or "we're shook" — never that register.
- **Restrained exclamation use.** One per page/email max, usually on a CTA, a tagline ("stepping up!"), or a warm sign-off ("Thanks so much!"). Five exclamation points in a caption = off-brand.
- **Specific over flowery.** "1-5 mile run or walk at any pace + drinks after" is the model. Concrete details, no marketing fluff. Numbers and verbs over adjectives.
- **Italicized emphasis instead of all-caps.** "_your_ run club" — italics carry the weight that hype-brands give to ALL CAPS. (This holds even in sentence-case email copy — still no shouting.)
- **You / we contrast pairs do structural work.** "you bring X. we bring Y." Same move as "ready for miles or mimosas" — paired contrast over single-claim assertion.
- **Casual + grown.** They use "drinks after" and "love people," but they also use "build something from the ground up" and "real ownership." Adult-casual, not college-casual.

## Words they do NOT use (or use rarely)

- "elite," "performance" (without context), "athlete" (as a generic flex)
- "disrupt," "movement" (as marketing word), "revolutionize"
- "girlboss," "queen," "babe," "y'all," "obsessed," "literally," "iconic"
- "level up," "unlock," "transform," "elevate"

- Generic running clichés: "go the distance," "push your limits," "no excuses"
- Health/wellness jargon overload: "biohack," "high-performance" (rare)
- Excessive hashtags. They're light on hashtags in the visible copy.

## Voice rules for Cowork to follow

When generating any draft *as Cool Down*:

1. **Pick capitalization by surface first.** Check the table above. Site/social/product → lowercase. Email/DM/customer reply → sentence case. If the surface isn't clear from the prompt, ask before drafting.
2. **One feeling per sentence; one exclamation per piece.** Don't stack energy.
3. **Specific over universal.** "Tuesdays at 6:30, 1-5 miles, drinks after" beats "join the run club community."
4. **Pair contrast as a structural move.** "you bring X. we bring Y." / "miles or mimosas." / "founder energy, but with support."
5. **Italicized emphasis over caps** — even in sentence-case emails. No shouting on any surface.
6. **Hype-language test.** Read the draft. If it could appear on any other apparel/community brand's IG or a generic partnerships email, rewrite. Cool Down sounds like Cool Down — friendly, grounded, specific.
7. **Numbers > adjectives.** "1-5 miles," "15+ cities," "Tuesday 6:30" — concrete details signal real over performative.
8. **Friend-not-influencer voice.** They write like a friend who organizes the run, not like a brand trying to sound like a friend. In email surfaces, this becomes "warm-professional" — like a friend who's also asking a real favor.

## Voice rules for *critique* (not generation)

When Cowork is critiquing a draft from Bailey or Anya:

- Quote the specific line that's drifting, then propose a sharper version *in Cool Down voice*.
- Never say "consider tightening" — produce the tightened version.
- If a draft uses ALL CAPS for emphasis, propose the italicized version.
- If a draft has more than one exclamation point, name it and cut one.
- If a draft sounds like a generic running brand, name which line specifically and rewrite using rule #4 (pair contrast) or rule #7 (numbers over adjectives).

## What "sounds like Cool Down" looks like in one paragraph

**For an Instagram caption after a run** (lowercase surface):

> *tuesday in denver. 14 of us, 3 miles, one questionable gas-station coffee. you don't need to be fast — you do need to show up. see you next week 🤍*

That's the target. Lowercase. Specific (city, count, distance, the coffee detail). Pair contrast (you don't need X, you do need Y). One emoji, no exclamation. The tag at the end is small and warm, not pushy.

**For a chapter launch invite to a recurring sponsor** (sentence-case email surface):

> Hi Nicholas! I hope you are well. We are launching our second college-targeted club in Ann Arbor for University of Michigan on October 27th and we were wondering if Garmin would like to be a part of our launch? We would love to have you out!
> Thanks so much!

Sentence case. Standard "Hi" greeting with the contact's first name. One sentence on what we're doing (city + school + date + which # college club it is). One sentence asking. Warm close. Two exclamations max — they read as warmth, not hype, in this register. Same voice DNA as the lowercase caption — short, specific, friendly, no fluff — just dressed for an inbox.

## What "doesn't sound like Cool Down" looks like

> *🔥🔥 ABSOLUTELY UNREAL energy at last night's Denver run!! 🤩 Our INCREDIBLE community came together for an EPIC 5-mile journey under the stars 🌙✨ This is what we LIVE for! Don't miss next week! Link in bio 👀💪 #running #runclub #motivation #fitness #fitfam #runners #runninglife #runlife*

Every sentence breaks at least three voice rules. Cowork should never produce something this shape, even when prompted casually.
