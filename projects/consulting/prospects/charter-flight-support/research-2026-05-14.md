## Executive read

Charter Flight Support is a private aviation support company focused on AOG recovery for Part 135 charter brokers and operators. Their core promise is simple: when an aircraft goes mechanical, CFS helps source replacement lift and contributes toward the replacement cost difference so the broker or operator can protect the client experience without absorbing a painful last-minute delta.

Their current public voice has moved toward a more mature B2B operations posture: calm, direct, financially literate, and centered on client trust. Their social/event voice is warmer and more relationship-heavy, with casual industry language, event energy, and founder-led friendliness. The opportunity is not to "invent" a voice. It is to document the voice system so their team can use the right register for each channel: emergency support, sales education, FAQ, newsletter, LinkedIn, event copy, and partner follow-up.

## What they sell

CFS describes itself as "24/7 AOG recovery support for Part 135 charter." The operating model is:

- Brokers/operators add CFS support when booking a charter.
- If the assigned aircraft goes mechanical, the client notifies CFS.
- CFS begins sourcing immediately, presents recovery options, and contributes toward the replacement aircraft cost difference.
- The broker/operator remains client-facing while CFS handles recovery logistics behind the scenes.

Key claims and mechanics:

- Built for Part 135 charter brokers and operators.
- Focused on unscheduled mechanical events within 10 days of departure.
- Not positioned as insurance. Their FAQ and pricing pages repeatedly call it a support service or membership model.
- Pricing is percentage-based and tied to original trip value. Current public page shows member pricing around 2.5% to 6.5% of original trip value, with market-based non-member rates.
- They frame the pain as three pressures: client relationship pressure, replacement cost exposure, and last-minute sourcing burden.

## Company structure and ecosystem

CFS is a product of Aviation Innovation Holdings, LLC. AIH describes itself as a diversified holding company focused on creating, building, acquiring, and scaling differentiated businesses in private aviation. AIH lists CFS and Falcon MGA Services' JetSure product in its portfolio.

This matters for the pitch: CFS is not just a scrappy service desk. The public footprint suggests a growing aviation venture platform with technology, data, insurance-adjacent products, events, and private aviation relationships. A voice/context system should be designed to scale across CFS first, then potentially across AIH portfolio companies later.

## Voice diagnosis

The brand has two useful voices that need a documented switchboard:

- Operational voice: calm, precise, trust-preserving. Used for website, FAQ, pricing, sales collateral, onboarding, AOG process, support rules.
- Community voice: casual, peer-to-peer, industry-insider, warm. Used for LinkedIn, events, partner shoutouts, newsletter intros, relationship maintenance.

The strongest strategic voice territory:

"Calm inside aviation chaos."

They do not sound like luxury travel marketing. They sound like aviation operators who understand what happens at 2 a.m. when the plane goes mechanical and a high-value client is waiting. The emotional layer is not glamour; it is relief, continuity, and protected reputation.

## Watchouts

- Avoid calling the product insurance unless CFS explicitly does. Their current CFS pages distinguish the support service from insurance.
- Be careful with reimbursement language. Use "contributes toward the cost difference" or "covers the cost difference up to defined limits" only when anchored to CFS-approved terms.
- Do not imply CFS controls flight operations or directly operates aircraft.
- Keep client/passenger safety claims conservative.
- Do not over-luxury the brand. This is an operational risk and continuity product, not a lifestyle brand.
- For AOG communications, AI should draft only. A human should approve anything client-facing or crisis-adjacent.
