# /speaking-prep

**When to use:** Before a talk — keynote, parent night, graduation speech, podcast guest appearance.

**How to use:** Paste this prompt, then paste the event details and whatever notes exist. This skill should make the talk more embodied, less concept-heavy, and use Marta's accumulated podcast memory without simply repeating podcast material.

---

## Prompt to paste

```
/speaking-prep

I'm preparing for: [name the venue, audience, length, what's the ask]

Here's what I have so far:

[paste outline / draft / notes]

Critique it as my private editor. I want this talk to create a visceral experience in the room, not just teach a good idea.

Do NOT start with long critique paragraphs.
Do NOT produce a finished talk outline yet.
Start by helping me think out loud about the room, the people, and the moment I want to create.

Use this exact process:

1. MY QUICK READ
   2 to 4 sentences max.
   Tell me what you think the talk is trying to do.
   Name what feels most alive and what feels least specific.

2. QUESTIONS FOR ME
   Ask 5 to 7 specific questions.
   Keep each question short: 1 to 3 sentences max.
   Make them conversational and practical, like you are helping me prepare in the hour before rehearsal.

   Your questions should help me name:
   - who is actually in the room
   - what they are carrying when they arrive
   - what I want them to feel physically by the end
   - the one moment or story that could make the talk land
   - what I should ask the room
   - what podcast thread could be useful here, and what would feel recycled
   - what I personally believe enough to say plainly

   Good question shape:
   - "What do you want the quietest person in the room to feel but never say out loud?"
   - "What is the moment in this talk where the room should go still?"
   - "Is there a story from a podcast episode that belongs here, or would that feel imported?"
   - "What do you want parents to do differently in the car on the way home?"
   - "What line would you be nervous to say because it is too true?"

   Bad question shape:
   - broad strategy questions
   - long diagnostic paragraphs
   - generic advice about storytelling
   - a full outline before I have answered

3. WAIT
   Stop after the questions.
   Wait for my answers.

After I answer, give me this exact output:

1. THE AUDIENCE IN ONE LINE
   Who is actually in this room? (Not who I wish were there — who *is*.)
   What do they walk in already believing? What do they need to feel by the end?

2. THE ROOM MOMENT
   What is the moment I want in the room?
   Name it physically: silence, laughter, tears, a throat catch, a hand raised, parents looking at their child differently, students sitting up straighter.
   If my current outline does not create that moment, say so.

3. THE VISCERAL HOOK
   Where in this talk does the audience *feel* something physically — chest, throat, gut?
   If the answer is "nowhere," propose 2 specific moments where I could plant one.
   Use sensory language, real situations, not concepts.

4. THE STRUCTURAL READ
   - What is the one sentence this talk is actually arguing?
   - Where does the tension build? Where does it drop?
   - What's the section I'd cut if I had to lose 5 minutes?
   - What feels too abstract — name the exact sentence.

5. PODCAST MEMORY CROSS-POLLINATION
   Check content-memory.md and podcast-truths.md.
   Give me:
   - 2 podcast threads I could bring into this talk in a fresh way
   - 1 podcast thread I should avoid because I've used it too much or it does not fit this room
   - 1 question from past podcast work that could become an audience question here
   Do not turn this into a podcast recap. Translate the wisdom for this room.

6. OPENING + CLOSING
   - Rate my current opening 1-10 and say why.
   - Rewrite it once, in my voice, sharper.
   - Same for the closing line. The last sentence is the thing they leave with.

7. THE QUESTIONS I SHOULD ASK THE ROOM
   Three questions I could ask the audience that would make this less of a teach and more of a shared experience.
   At least one should be quiet enough that they answer only inside themselves.
   At least one should be concrete enough that they can picture a real person or moment.

8. WHAT TO PRACTICE OUT LOUD
   5 to 8 bullets I should rehearse.
   These are not script lines. They are turns, images, transitions, or questions I cannot afford to lose.

9. WHAT TO SEND GRACE
   If I'm pre-recording or there's video, what should I tell Grace to look for in the edit?

End with: ONE line — what would make this talk land harder, if I changed nothing else?
```

---

## Notes on running this

- If the talk is for a high-stakes venue (return invite, paid speaking), run the prompt twice — once cold, once after you've revised based on the first pass. The second pass catches drift.
- If the answer feels generic, paste 2–3 more pages of source material and ask it to redo section 3. Quality of critique scales with quantity of context.
- Use `content-memory.md` as compost, not as a script bank. The goal is to let Marta's thinking compound while still making each talk specific to the room.
- If Cowork starts producing paragraphs instead of a conversation, paste: "Shorter. Ask me specific questions first."
- Push back if you disagree. The Project is calibrated to take pushback and revise.
