## Format profile

- **Solo + guest mix.** ~70% solo reflection, ~30% guest conversations (Denise on faith/medical crisis; Wendy Angst on life design; Deanna Gumpf on D1 coaching).
- **Variable length.** 9 minutes (shortest in sample) to 30 minutes (longest). She's not locked to a runtime — episode length follows the idea.
- **Two-part arcs are her signature move.** 4 of the 10 most recent episodes are Part 1 / Part 2 splits. She thinks in arcs, not single episodes. ("How We Learned to Love" Part 1+2; "Voice Memos and Siblinghood" Part 1+2; "Designing Your Life" Part 1+2.)
- **Cadence:** officially biweekly, in practice running ~weekly for the last 6 weeks. (Watch this — Marta named the cadence drift in discovery; possibly wearing on her.)
- **Former name:** *How to Human*. Renamed to *The Practice of Being Human*. The shift from "how to" → "practice of" is meaningful — she moved away from the prescriptive frame.

## Pulled lines (her actual phrasing — verified from show notes)

These are from her own descriptions and used as voice anchors:

- *"Wounds are also the places where our greatest gifts were formed."*
- *"Confidence is not 'I always win.' It is 'I can handle what comes.'"*
- *"Meaningful healing often happens not through dramatic breakthroughs, but through consistent, honest moments of being heard and understood."*
- *"When our perspective shifts, the meaning of our past begins to change."*

These four lines confirm the voice rules in `voice.md`:
- Aphoristic but specific
- Contrast pairs do structural work ("not X / it is Y")
- "Quiet" and "consistent" valued over "dramatic" and "transformational"
- Past as material, not as wound to be fixed

## What this podcast does that nothing else does (provisional)

Based on theme clustering + arc structure:

1. **Two-part arc treatment.** Most podcasts in this space publish single episodes. Marta's Part 1 / Part 2 structure suggests she trusts her audience to sit with an idea over a week. This is a craft choice — protect it.
2. **Frame migration over time.** "How to Human" → "The Practice of Being Human" is a real evolution. She's moved from prescription to practice, and the show notes consistently use "practice," "noticing," "sit with" rather than "do this," "fix that."
3. **Slow-podcast posture.** Episode lengths 9–30 min, no signs of forcing length, no "we'll dive deep" hype language in descriptions. The show notes themselves are restrained — they don't oversell.
4. **Personal material as the substrate, not the entertainment.** Voice Memos arc is *about* her brother. The Denise episode is *about* Denise's grief. The personal is doing analytic work, not just hooking listeners.

## Drift patterns to watch for (provisional — needs LISTEN-PASS verification)

Inferred from theme clustering + show notes:

1. **Over-reliance on Maté/Perel as frame.** Easy default. Cowork should flag when a new idea is being explained *through* one of them rather than on its own terms.
2. **Family-of-origin saturation.** 5 of 10 recent episodes touch family-of-origin. If it's her strongest material, fine — but `/podcast-pressure-test` should ask whether the *new* episode is doing something her last 5 didn't.
3. **The "perspective shift" frame, repeated.** Two of the recent 10 episodes argue versions of "your perspective on the past determines its meaning." Strong idea, but if Episode 192 also pivots on it, name that.
4. **Two-part episodes that should be one-part.** `LISTEN-PASS: confirm whether Part 2s consistently earn their length, or whether they sometimes echo Part 1 with diminished tension.` This is a real risk for the format she's chosen.
5. **Aphoristic lines as substitute for structure.** Her aphorisms are strong (see pulled lines above). But aphorisms can paper over weak underlying argument. Podcast review should test: if you stripped the aphoristic lines, would the episode still hold?
