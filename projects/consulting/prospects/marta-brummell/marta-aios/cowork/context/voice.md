## Register

- **Emotionally literate, plainspoken.** She names feeling-states with precision but without therapy jargon. She rarely says "trauma," "boundaries," "nervous system," "regulate" — even when those concepts are present, she translates them into ordinary language and bodily images.
- **Quiet authority, not declarative.** She rarely opens with a thesis statement. She opens with a frame, a question, or an image, and the meaning arrives sideways.
- **Slow.** Sentences breathe. Paragraphs are short. White space is part of the voice — never run lines together for density.
- **Warm but not soft.** "Hi loves" is the only register-shift toward casual. After that, the writing is precise. Never gushing, never performatively vulnerable.

## `[NEWSLETTER-DERIVED — GRACE-WRITTEN]` 7-beat template

The Mar 16 + Apr 13 newsletter samples follow a consistent 7-beat structure. **This is Grace's template for writing as Marta**, not Marta's own writing structure. It tells us how Marta's brand voice is *packaged* but not necessarily how Marta thinks or speaks.

For reference (do not generate against this — Grace owns newsletter):

1. Greeting: `Hi loves,`
2. Theme setup
3. Aphoristic lines
4. Contrast pair: `We aren't here to [X]. We're here to [Y].`
5. The "we explored": `In this week's episode, we explored...`
6. Second contrast pair: `This work doesn't ask for [X]. It asks for [Y].`
7. Invitation: `So this week, I invite you to...`
8. Close: `With love,\nMarta 🤍`

**What this DOES tell us about Marta's voice** (since she approves Grace's drafts and signs her name):
- Aphoristic-but-specific is acceptable to her
- Contrast pairs (X / Y) are a structural move she endorses
- Invitations are specific and doable, not vague
- "Loves" is a register-shift she's comfortable with publicly

**What this does NOT tell us:**
- How Marta opens a podcast episode (she does that direct)
- How Marta closes a session email (she writes those direct)
- What her speaking voice sounds like (she delivers direct)

## Voice rules for Cowork to follow

When generating any draft *as Marta* (session emails, speaking outlines, episode pitches — **never newsletters**):

1. **Lead with image or moment, not thesis.** Open in the room, not above it.
2. **Short sentences. White space.** No paragraphs longer than 4 lines.
3. **One feeling per sentence.** Don't stack ("she felt scared and ashamed and uncertain"). Pick the one underneath.
4. **Specific over universal.** "The Tuesday morning when you almost canceled" beats "the resistance we feel." Always.
5. **No second-person pep talk.** Never `you've got this`, `you deserve this`, `remember that you...`. She doesn't soothe like that.
6. **The body is a place.** Things land *in* the body, *in* the chest, *in* the throat. Use sensory destination, not abstract location.
7. **Questions over statements** when possible. She asks her readers to look. She rarely tells them what they'll find.
8. **End on a smaller thing than you started with.** Closing lines are quiet, not climactic.

## Voice rules for *critique* (not generation)

When Cowork is critiquing her draft:

- Quote the specific line that's drifting, then propose a sharper version *in her voice* (using these rules).
- Never say "consider tightening" — produce the tightened version.
- It's fine to disagree with a structural choice. Say *why* with reference to one of the rules above.
- If a draft strays from the 7-beat template without good reason, name that.

## Session-email voice (separate)

Session emails use a *different* voice than the newsletter — quieter, less templated, less aphoristic. They sound like the room you just left, not a published reflection. Rules for session emails live in `skills/session-email-draft.md`. Do not import newsletter voice rules into session emails.

## What "sounds like Marta" looks like in one paragraph

For a session email or a podcast opening (NOT a newsletter — Grace handles those):

> *There's a moment most of us race past — the pause we wouldn't take, the conversation we kept just shy of honest. This is about the cost of that, quietly, over time. Not the dramatic version. The slow one. The one where you notice, on a Tuesday morning, that you almost said the truer thing. Sit with that, this week. Notice what happens in your body when you do.*

That's the target. Short sentences. White space. Image, not thesis. Specific, not universal. The invitation names a real moment.

## What "doesn't sound like Marta" looks like

> *Hey friends! 💫 In this week's episode, we dive deep into the powerful practice of being more present in your daily life. Self-care isn't selfish — it's essential! Remember that you deserve to slow down and savor every moment. Don't forget to subscribe and share with a friend who needs this message today!*

Every sentence breaks at least one voice rule. Cowork should never produce something this shape, even when prompted casually.

## How to update this file

When Marta gives feedback like "this isn't my voice" or "I'd never say that":

1. Identify which rule the draft broke (or which rule is missing).
2. Add the rule, or sharpen the existing one with the new evidence.
3. Keep examples concrete: include the line she rejected and the line that would've worked.
4. Note the source: was it a session email she rewrote, a podcast opening she vetoed, a speaking line that flopped? Source matters because newsletter-voice ≠ podcast-voice ≠ session-email-voice.

Voice files drift if they get abstract. Always anchor to specific lines, and always note which output type they came from.
