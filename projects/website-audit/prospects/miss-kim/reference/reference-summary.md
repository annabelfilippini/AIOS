# Reference Sites — Miss Kim Ann Arbor
Chosen 2026-04-19 by Annabel. Each ref scraped to `reference/<brand>/` and its `homepage-desktop-full.png` was visually inspected for this summary.

## Rare Bird Rooftop — https://www.rarebirdrooftop.com/

**Why chosen:** Annabel liked the warm, handwritten-wordmark editorial feel of a multi-city hospitality brand — understated, not shouty.

**Execution takeaways for Miss Kim:**
- **Typography:** Single hero typographic moment — the "RareBird" wordmark sits in a cursive, red-orange script at the very top, centered and small. Section labels ("nashville tennessee", "denver colorado") are set in a light serif, cream-colored, italic-feeling, overlaid on full-bleed photos. No sans-serif body is visible above the fold — the whole page reads as typographic restraint.
- **Color palette:** Pale dusty-pink / blush ground (~#F6E4DD), red-orange accent for the wordmark (~#E94E2F), and the photo-bottom marquee strip. Cream-white for image labels. Only three colors do all the work.
- **Hero treatment:** No hero "statement" — the entire first screen is a two-image picker: Nashville (cocktail/guest photo) on the left, Denver (pizza being pulled from oven) on the right, both inset with generous pink margin around them. Each image carries its city name in cream italic serif. Feels like a magazine contents page.
- **Layout patterns:** Generous pink gutter around every asset. Images are inset rectangles, not full-bleed. A red-orange footer marquee strip anchors the bottom. No horizontal rules — whitespace does the separation.
- **Photography:** Candid guest + food photos with a warm color grade — a hand holding a cocktail, hands pulling a pizza. Not shot-for-website; feels found.
- **Motion:** None visible on the captured screen (static picker).
- **Social proof:** None above the fold — the brand leans on photography for trust, not logos.
- **Nav:** Tiny — "Back | Nashville | Denver" top-left, "Back | Denver | Nashville" top-right. Minimal chrome. No sticky header visible.

**Do NOT copy:** The two-city picker homepage — Miss Kim is single-location. The pink blush palette — it's too brunch-y for a Korean restaurant leaning into fermentation/ferment warmth. The cursive wordmark logo style — Miss Kim's existing sans-logo wordmark is part of its identity.

## Bull & Last — https://thebullandlast.co.uk/ (and /pages/menus)

**Why chosen:** Gastropub with a grown-up, editorial homepage — and a menus page that is genuinely beautiful, not a PDF dump.

**Execution takeaways for Miss Kim:**
- **Typography:** Serif display headers for section titles ("A PUB WITH GUEST ROOMS IN NORTH LONDON", "MENUS", "ROOMS", "SHOP") set in a slim, all-caps engraved serif. Body copy is a warm, book-weight sans — small, centered, generous leading. No Montserrat, no Lato — this is hand-selected.
- **Color palette:** Deep teal / hunter green (~#1E4A47) for the nav bar and footer; warm cream / bone (~#F3EEE4) for page ground; muted clay/terracotta accents in illustrations. The menus page has that teal as a full-bleed hero strip over a wide food photo. Serif-and-green is the signature.
- **Hero treatment:** Homepage hero is a dim interior/bar photo with the site name tucked top-center in a cream badge, headline typed over the photo in narrow all-caps serif. Menus page uses a full-bleed overhead table shot with the teal strip — communal shared-dining energy.
- **Layout patterns:** Centered single-column rhythm. Short intro paragraph under each H2. Pill-shaped menu links (Lunch & Dinner, Sunday, Pie & Pint Night, Afternoon Menu, Wine List) stacked in two rows of buttons — that's how you expose menu variety without forcing a PDF click until intent is clear. Follow with a long vertical column of dish photos, each the full content width, plenty of breathing room between them.
- **Photography:** Dark, moody, plated, overhead-and-3/4 mix. Every plate shot on the same wood table with the same lighting — deeply consistent. This is "one photographer, one day" energy, not stock.
- **Motion:** None above the fold on the still. Instagram grid loops at the bottom.
- **Social proof:** "Follow us on Instagram @thebullandlast" — the feed itself IS the social proof. No press logos at all. A "careers" paragraph reads like a founder note.
- **Nav:** Very thin — "Make A Reservation" top-left as a text link, cream wordmark centered, cart top-right. No dropdown mega-menus.

**Do NOT copy:** Guest-rooms block and the cookbook/tote/gin shop — Miss Kim is single-concept dining with no retail and no lodging. The British-colonial naming (Somerset, Keats, Dido) is deeply tied to a Hampstead Heath location story.

## King Restaurant NYC — https://kingrestaurant.nyc/

**Why chosen:** Fine-ish dining with absolute restraint — the site quietly communicates "we know what we're doing."

**Execution takeaways for Miss Kim:**
- **Typography:** Slim serif wordmark at the very top ("King Restaurant" with address underneath in tiny caps). Section labels ("ABOUT", "HOURS & LOCATION", "RESERVATIONS") are set as small bold all-caps sans — label-sized, not heading-sized. The body of the About paragraph is set as an oversized book-weight serif — the copy itself is the design element. It's a typographic restraint move.
- **Color palette:** Cream / off-white page ground (~#F3EDE2), near-black serif type, no accent color at all. The images do the color work. This is literally two-color design.
- **Hero treatment:** The site opens with a small centered wordmark and a cookbook announcement line, then three stacked full-width photos (dining room, table spread, bar room). No big H1, no tagline, no CTA above the fold. You learn what this place is by scrolling through its rooms.
- **Layout patterns:** Centered single-column, photos always full content width and equal height. Section blocks = label in caps, then one big paragraph of prose. Hours are a run of lines ("DINNER Seven nights a week…") — formatted like a letter, not a grid.
- **Photography:** White tablecloths, muted-tone dining rooms, overhead table spreads with Mediterranean-looking plates, warm incandescent light, wine-being-poured candids. Zero color grading — feels like natural daylight. This is the "photography IS the design" lesson.
- **Motion:** None. This is a stone-still site — even the nav doesn't stick.
- **Social proof:** A Pete Wells 2018 NYT review quote buried inside the About paragraph, fenced with em-dashes ("— it is both a neighborhood restaurant as well as a celebratory destination"). A critic quote as prose, not a pull-quote box.
- **Nav:** Top-center wordmark, no traditional menu visible on the still — they trust the scroll.

**Do NOT copy:** The white-tablecloth fine-dining aesthetic — Miss Kim is trendy/casual/moderate-noise per Yelp, not hushed. The zero-CTA homepage — Miss Kim needs OpenTable and Order CTAs visible. No-footer-nav would be inaccessible for a restaurant with confusing parking entrance (the Miss Kim parking copy is load-bearing).

## La Semilla — https://www.lasemilla.kitchen/

**Why chosen:** Farm-to-table / plant-based brand system with strong illustration and palette work — different vibe, similar "Latin American" parallel to Miss Kim's "Korean-inspired with local Michigan ingredients" positioning.

**Execution takeaways for Miss Kim:**
- **Typography:** Heavy custom lockup — stacked serif + script logo with palm-tree illustration baked in. Section headers ("menu", "order online", "hours of operation", "Contact us") are set in a lowercase slab-slash-serif. Body copy is small, book-weight, often italicized Spanish phrases ("sólo plantas", "sit back, relax, & enjoy the possibilities,,.") — the bilingual voice IS the brand.
- **Color palette:** This is the most polychromatic of the four refs. Pine green (~#264B35) for primary type and illustrations, terracotta / burnt orange (~#D86A3E) for the order-online section, soft peach (~#F3C79C) for hero ground, cream (~#EDE4C9) and olive (~#8E8944). Four-color restaurant branding, applied with discipline.
- **Hero treatment:** The first screen is a full-width food shot (tortilla close-up, warm tones), label-sized "sólo plantas" tag above it, small cursive type "modern latin cuisine" below, single green "menu" link. The full brand system reveals as you scroll — the hero is just the entrance.
- **Layout patterns:** Alternating color-blocks — cream section, then terracotta section, then peach section. Each block does one job (intro, menu gallery, hours, contact). The menu is displayed as six clickable IMAGE panels (printed menu pages rendered like editorial spreads), not as HTML body text or a PDF link.
- **Photography:** Overhead shots of plated food, natural light, warm color grade. Candid plates — not hero dishes isolated on backdrops.
- **Motion:** Subtle scroll — the color blocks reveal as you descend. Instagram grid at the bottom rotates recent posts in full-size squares.
- **Social proof:** "La Semilla is the flagship concept by Happy Seed, grown from Atlanta, Georgia." — the sister-concept + location story IS the credibility. Plus the Instagram grid. No press logos.
- **Nav:** Minimal — logo lockup top-center, tiny utility links. Everything navigates via scroll.

**Do NOT copy:** The palm-tree illustration in the lockup — Miss Kim has its own wordmark identity. The heavy bilingual Spanish-English voice — Miss Kim's voice is English-forward with Korean dish names only.

## Shared DNA across all 4

The things ALL four sites absolutely do that Miss Kim should absorb:

1. **Typography does the heavy lifting, not UI chrome.** Every one of the four sites uses a distinctive serif for display plus restrained sans/serif body. None uses Lato. None uses Montserrat. None uses gradient text or oversized hero fonts with stroke effects. The section label / label caps + long book-weight body paragraph rhythm is the consistent move.

2. **One signature color + generous neutral ground.** Rare Bird = red-orange on blush. Bull & Last = teal on cream. King = near-black on cream (no accent). La Semilla = pine green + terracotta on cream. Miss Kim's current bright blue (#1191B8) is a correct instinct (single accent), but the background should be a warm cream, not flat white, and the accent should feel pigment-ed rather than screen-hex.

3. **Photography is the identity.** Every site puts real food / real space / real people in the hero territory — not illustrations, not stock, not icons. Bull & Last and King even use photography in place of an H1 tagline. Miss Kim already has `jamesbeard.jpg` and the plated-food assets on the homepage — these should be enlarged and featured, not buried as tile thumbnails.

4. **Instagram loop, not press logos.** Bull & Last, La Semilla, and Rare Bird all close the page with an Instagram grid. None of them display an "As Seen In" logo bar. Miss Kim has @misskimannarbor and monthly chef pop-up programming — the feed is the press proof.

## Anti-patterns (things NONE of these do)

Things all four refs avoid, that AI mockups default to:

- **No drop-shadowed card components.** Zero box-shadow on any content block across all four. Cards divide content by whitespace + color-block, never by elevated boxes.
- **No "Our Story / Menu / Gallery / Contact" nav bar.** Nav is either nearly invisible (King, Rare Bird) or used for the single action that matters (Bull & Last = "Make A Reservation"). Miss Kim's current five-item Toast-site nav is the wrong pattern.
- **No three-column feature grids with icon + one-word + lorem.** None of these sites uses an icon-and-label grid at all.
- **No gradients.** Zero. Backgrounds are flat warm neutrals — never a fade.
- **No tight drop-cap paragraphs or SaaS "value prop" copy.** Body text reads like a restaurant voice ("we are always on the look out for talented individuals…"), not a SaaS landing page ("Elevate your dining experience").
- **No emojis.** None across all four reference sites. Reconfirms the global "no emojis on websites" rule.
- **No centered giant modal CTA stripe.** Reservation CTAs live in the nav and in a small button inside the intro paragraph — never as a full-bleed banner.
- **No carousel testimonial widget.** King embeds its Pete Wells quote in running prose; Bull & Last and La Semilla use the IG grid instead of testimonials; Rare Bird omits social proof entirely.
