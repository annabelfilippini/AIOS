Your Site Report

What You're Doing Right

Before we get into what to fix — Adelitas has something most Denver restaurants don't: a real story, a real chef, and a real point of view.

Your brand has a soul. The "La Adelita" heritage — the Michoacán roots, the woman-warrior iconography, the soldadera narrative — is one of the strongest brand stories I've seen on any restaurant site. It's authentic, specific, and emotionally resonant. A lot of Mexican restaurants in Denver trade on generic "auténtico" signals. Adelitas has a real one, tied to a real place, a real family, and a real chef.

Chef Silvia Andaya is a marketing asset most operators would kill for. A Michoacán-lineage chef running three Denver properties — Adelitas, La Doña, and Ni Tuyo — is a story that writes itself. Westword, 5280, Eater Denver, Uncover Colorado all run these pieces regularly. That story hasn't been told yet.

The Broadway location's atmosphere comes through. The evening-cantina photography (warm lighting, communal tables, festive but grounded) earns real trust with anyone who lands on that page. Edgewater has its own distinct feel — brighter, more modern. These are two genuine restaurants with two genuine identities.

La Doña is a legitimate hidden-gem. A mezcal-forward cocktail bar tucked behind Adelitas, chef-led, with an Oaxacan spirits focus — that positioning is gold. It doesn't need to be sanitized into a generic bar. It needs to be findable.

The names that show up instead are Casa Bonita, Tamayo, Los Chingones, Alma Fonda Fina, and Ni Tuyo (which, you may know, is your own third property).

Your Homepage Isn't A Homepage

This is the single biggest issue in the audit.

When anyone — a customer, a food critic, a travel blogger, Google itself — goes to adelitasco.com, here's what they find:

  • A page titled "Location Picker"
  • No description of the restaurant
  • No photos of the food
  • No mention of Chef Silvia
  • No mention of Michoacán
  • No "La Adelita" heritage
  • No reservation link
  • No menu preview
  • No link to La Doña or Ni Tuyo
  • Two cards: "Broadway" and "Edgewater"

Every backlink from every press mention, every email signature, every social post, every Yelp profile — they all land on a page that is, functionally, a blank chooser. Google has nothing to rank. Customers have nothing to read. The Michoacán story stays hidden.

The fix: rebuild adelitasco.com/ as a real homepage. The location chooser ("Broadway" / "Edgewater") moves into the header navigation where it belongs. The body becomes the actual restaurant: a hero image of the food or the room, the La Adelita heritage in two sentences, a menu preview, reservation CTAs for both locations, a Chef Silvia section, press quotes, and a footer that cross-links La Doña and Ni Tuyo.

That one change unlocks every other SEO finding in this audit.

The SEO Pages Are A Time Bomb

Adelitas currently has 17 pages in its sitemap with URLs like:

  • /best-mexican-food-in-denver-colorado
  • /best-mexican-restaurant-in-denver
  • /mezcal-in-denver
  • /tequila-restaurant-in-denver
  • /best-margaritas-in-denver
  • /best-brunch-in-denver-colorado
  • /best-authentic-tacos-in-denver
  • …and 10 more

These are called "doorway pages" in SEO — pages built specifically to rank on one keyword. Each of yours has 5–6 <h1> tags on a single page, 25,000 characters of keyword-stuffed narrative, 15 images with no alt text, and identical structured data copy-pasted across every URL.

Google's doorway-page policy explicitly describes this pattern. If caught in a manual review, it can trigger a site-wide ranking demotion — meaning every Adelitas page loses visibility, not just the doorway pages.

Here's the catch: these pages are currently your entire ranking presence. They are working — that's why they exist. If you just delete them, Adelitas goes dark on Google for three to six months while the rest of the site catches up.

The fix is a consolidation plan, not a deletion. Instead of 17 keyword-stuffed pages, you build 4 legitimate content hubs and 301-redirect the doorway URLs into them:

New HubReplaces
/menu (real HTML, linked from main nav)Your current /qr-code-menu empty stub
/our-story/tequilas-family-mexican-restaurant — Silvia + Michoacán + La Adelita, lifted out of the SEO URL
/tequilas-and-mezcalThe tequila / mezcal / cocktail / margaritas doorways
/brunchThe brunch / breakfast-burrito doorways
Rebuilt homepage /The generic "best Mexican food Denver" doorways

You keep the keywords. You lose the Google policy risk. The content becomes pages people actually want to read.

Your Reservations Are Hiding

People are trying to make reservations at Adelitas and can't.

Google Autocomplete data shows that "adelitas denver reservations" is the 4th most-typed search after the branded name. Your OpenTable listing exists — it's live right now at opentable.com/r/adelitas-cocina-y-cantina-denver — but it is not linked from anywhere on the Adelitas website. No "Reservations" button in the nav. No /reservations page. No mention in the footer.

Anyone landing on the site who wants to book has to leave the site and search for you on OpenTable directly. Many of them won't.

The fix is 15 minutes: add a "Reservations" button to the Adelitas header nav, link both locations to OpenTable. A one-click fix to a problem that's quietly costing you bookings.

Your Chef Is Your Brand And Nobody Knows Her

Chef Silvia Andaya has zero Google Autocomplete suggestions when someone starts typing her name. One editorial mention (Shoutout Colorado) is all that's indexed. For a Michoacán-lineage chef running three Denver restaurants, that's the most underleveraged asset in this audit.

The pieces of the story that are already written are buried inside an SEO page (/tequilas-family-mexican-restaurant) instead of surfaced as an editorial chef page. Press doesn't read SEO URLs — they read Chef pages.

The fix: build /chef as a dedicated editorial page. Full bio, Michoacán lineage, photography, press quotes, cross-links to Adelitas / La Doña / Ni Tuyo. Add Person schema so Google can build a proper knowledge graph entry. Then pitch Westword, 5280, Eater Denver, and Uncover Colorado on the angle: "One chef, three Denver restaurants, one Michoacán lineage." That story is unpitched and un-published.

One editorial hit in a publication like Westword is worth more in domain authority than the entire 17-page doorway cluster.

Five Things to Fix This Week

These are high-impact, low-effort. Each is under an hour.

1. Link OpenTable From the Header Nav

Add a "Reservations" button. Link it to your existing OpenTable page for both Broadway and Edgewater. Unmet customer intent, zero engineering cost.

2. Cross-Link Adelitas, La Doña, and Ni Tuyo

Right now none of the three restaurants link to each other. Add a footer block on each site: "Also from the Andaya family — [the other two]." Free domain-authority transfer across all three properties.

3. Fix the Form Bug on /catering

The /catering page currently shows <h1>Success!</h1> as part of the static page — a form-success message leaking into the main render. Anyone landing there thinks something's wrong. One-line Showit template fix.

4. Add a robots.txt to Both Domains

Neither adelitasco.com nor ladonamezcaleria.com has a robots.txt. This is a 2-line file that tells Google how to crawl the site. Without it, Google guesses.

5. Pin the Sip & Savor Event on the Homepage

The April Sip & Savor tequila dinner with Chef Silvia and Adolfo Aguilar is the kind of content that earns reservations and press. It's on its own page. It's not on the homepage. Anyone who lands on adelitasco.com sees a location picker, not the event. Pin it until the dinner is full.

Where AI Fits In

You don't need a bigger marketing team. You need sharper infrastructure. Here's where AI tools make the biggest difference for Adelitas specifically:

Editorial pitching at scale. AI can draft ten versions of the Silvia + three-restaurant pitch for Westword, 5280, Eater Denver, Uncover Colorado, and Denver Post — each personalized to the publication's voice and recent coverage. You review, you send. One landed pitch earns you more organic traffic than a year of doorway pages.

Menu as crawlable content. Your current menu lives inside Showit galleries that Google can't read. AI can transcribe your menu images into structured HTML with proper Menu schema, so dishes show up as rich results on Google and in voice search.

Reviews curation across three properties. Between Adelitas, La Doña, and Ni Tuyo, you have hundreds of Google, Yelp, and OpenTable reviews scattered across three listings. AI can pull the best quotes, categorize by experience (Taco Tuesday, private events, mezcal flights, first-date energy), and publish as a curated reviews hub on each site.

Press-ready chef bio. A one-time AI-drafted Chef Silvia editorial page — bio, heritage, philosophy, press quotes — that then powers every pitch, every press kit, every Instagram about-page.

Event-program scaffolding. Sip & Savor shouldn't be a one-off. AI can maintain a recurring event hub (tequila pairings, Taco Tuesday, private dining) with schema markup so each event shows up in Google's event-rich-results panel.

The Housekeeping

A few smaller items worth addressing:

  • La Doña's URL situation is genuinely broken. The site's canonical points to a domain users can't type. We can fix this cleanly in a single engineering pass — 30 minutes total.
  • Broadway and Edgewater pages are identical. Same title, same photos, same structure — only the address differs. Google picks one, suppresses the other. Differentiate with separate photography, menu highlights, and review pulls. Each location earns its own personality.
  • /qr-code-menu has no content. The "Menus" button in your nav goes to an empty page. Rebuild as a real /menu.
  • The /catering page has a form bug leaking "Success!" into the main page render. Showit template fix.
  • The mobile homepage may not render. We detected a near-blank mobile screenshot of adelitasco.com — worth a manual DevTools check. If mobile is broken, it affects ~60% of restaurant-discovery traffic.
  • No robots.txt on either domain. 2-line file, meaningful for Google's crawler.
  • The "La Adelita" heritage narrative is hidden. It's the single strongest brand differentiator — lift it to the homepage and the chef page.
  • No blog or journal. Nothing to pitch to editorial. Nothing for Google to index as fresh. One post a month, written in AI-assisted drafts, is enough to change this.
