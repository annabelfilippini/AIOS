# Reference Sites — Amer's Deli

Chosen 2026-04-20 (from sitebuilderreport.com/inspiration/restaurant-websites + vertical match). Each ref was scraped to `reference/<brand>/` (may be pruned by /audit-cleanup — this file is the durable breadcrumb).

Positioning pick: **heritage-forward** — vintage typography, 1988/1990 anchor, archival/editorial feel over modern SaaS.

## Landini Brothers — https://www.landinibrothers.com/

Why chosen: Classic heritage-restaurant treatment. "Since 1979" hero, owner-named ("Your hosts, Franco and Noe Landini"), editorial food photography with visible photo credits, dark palette + amber CTAs. Closest match for the "Amer's, since 1988" heritage anchor.

Execution takeaways for Amer's:
- **Hero = a single oversized dish photo + a short heritage headline** ("Since 1988" / "On State Street since 1990"). No carousel, no hero video.
- **Display serif for H1s with a tight letter-form** — pairs well against a grotesque body. Landini uses Didone-feeling capitals; the Amer's version should be warmer (Playfair, Canela, or Recoleta — not cold fashion-magazine Didone).
- **Dark background bands alternate with cream** — lets food photography carry the energy. Amer's palette pivots from the current PopMenu dark-gray to deep espresso + warm ivory.
- **Owner-named block** — Landini calls out Franco + Noe by first name. Amer's equivalent: "You'll find Amer behind the counter" (verified Michigan Daily quote).
- **Photo credits visible** — signals editorial polish. We don't have named photographers for Amer's, so skip credits and let the images breathe instead.

Do NOT copy: Tuscan fine-dining cues — tripleseat events, Certified Angus Beef call-out, white tablecloth lighting. Amer's is counter-service campus-deli, not reservation-required trattoria. Keep the formality OUT.

## High Street Deli — https://www.highstdeli.com/

Why chosen: Same vertical (counter-service deli / sando shop), confident neighborhood identity, hand-drawn illustrated wordmark, playful-but-specific menu voice. The deli-vertical execution complement to Landini's heritage cues.

Execution takeaways for Amer's:
- **Named sandos with specific copy** (High Street: "California Turkey," "Dutch Punch," "Nashville Club"). Amer's has real named items too — **#101 Eggwich, Amazon Bowl, Power Açaí Bowl, Chicago Reds hot dog, Reuben.** Use the names; don't genericize.
- **Price on the menu, visible from the mockup** — $14.50 printed under each sando. Builds trust, avoids the mystery-menu feel. Amer's should show Reuben $14.99 and a couple more prices inline.
- **Illustrated brand mark + badge system** — High Street's shield/SLO County crest gives it location identity. Amer's equivalent: elevate the existing hand-drawn **"welcome frog"** into a secondary badge/mascot rather than hiding it. (Per feedback: elevate signature elements, don't erase them.)
- **Copy voice has opinions** — "The Works = mayo, yellow or dijon…", "Make It Stoney!" Amer's voice should similarly own its quirks: Amer himself advises, menu spans half the restaurant, staff remembers your name.
- **Green + cream palette + oversized food hero**. Amer's palette should swap to espresso + ivory + a single brick-red or mustard accent (nods to Reuben / Chicago Reds / classic deli awning).

Do NOT copy: High Street's hand-lettered surfer cursive or the literal SLO County shield. That belongs to them. Keep Amer's mark rooted in Ann Arbor cues — a State Street / Diag reference, not a California badge.

## Combined brief for /audit-redesign

**Pull from Landini:** heritage anchor ("Since 1988"), oversized editorial food photography, owner-named block, dark-band/cream-band rhythm, display serif + grotesque body pairing.

**Pull from High Street:** counter-service deli voice (named items + prices), illustrated badge system, confident neighborhood identity, opinionated menu copy.

**Keep from Amer's itself (do not erase):**
- The **"welcome frog"** — weird but owned. Promote to mascot badge, not hero.
- **Chicago Reds** and **Yogurt Rush** as sub-brands — these are signatures, not menu noise.
- **"We are Amer's!"** as a tone anchor.
- Amer-himself-behind-the-counter as the human hook.
