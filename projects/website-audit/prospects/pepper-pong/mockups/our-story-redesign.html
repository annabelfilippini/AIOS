A Dream Held Captive — Then Set Free

For years, the idea for Pepper Pong lived only in Tom Filippini's head. A game that could turn any surface into a playing field. A way to bring people together — face to face, phones down, laughing. The concept was there. The clarity to build it was not.

Alcohol addiction held that dream captive. It wasn't until 2016, when Tom got sober, that everything changed. Sobriety didn't just give him his life back — it gave him the focus, the courage, and the deep belief in human connection that would become the soul of Pepper Pong.

"This game is a tribute to those who helped me achieve and maintain sobriety — including all who get in on the action."
— Tom Filippini, Founder

What started as a kitchen-table prototype became a real product — a portable paddle sport that sets up in 60 seconds, plays whisper-quiet, and turns skeptics into obsessives in a single rally. Tom poured years of iteration into getting it right: the paddles, the net, the ball, the carry case. Every piece designed for the moment someone picks it up for the first time.

The early days were scrappy. Tom sold sets out of his house, showed up at events, handed the paddles to anyone who'd play. Word of mouth did the rest. Once people played, they didn't stop talking about it.

In 2025, Pepper Pong walked onto the Shark Tank stage and walked out with a deal and a record. The show validated what thousands of players already knew: this game is different.

But the pitch wasn't just about the product. Tom shared his recovery story with the Sharks and millions of viewers. The vulnerability that built Pepper Pong became the thing that set it apart.

Rally 4 Recovery FIX — integrated, not tacked on

The Game That Gives Back

Pepper Pong was born from recovery. It only exists because Tom found sobriety. So from the very beginning, giving back to recovery communities hasn't been a "program" — it's been the whole point.

Rally 4 Recovery donates Pepper Pong sets to addiction recovery facilities across the country. Because the same thing that makes this game great — face-to-face connection, laughter, belonging — is exactly what recovery needs.

How It Works

  1. Recovery facility submits a donation request
  2. Pepper Pong ships a free set directly to the facility
  3. Patients play — connection defeats isolation
