## What You're Doing Right

Before we get into what to fix — Pepper Pong's site has real strengths that most small brands never figure out:

**Your brand voice is dialed in.** "Spicy," "Rally," "Peppers," "QUESTIONS YOUR MOM WOULD ASK" — the vocabulary is consistent, fun, and uniquely yours. Most Shopify stores sound like they were written by the same marketing intern. Pepper Pong doesn't.

**Your social proof is elite.** 211 reviews at 4.95 stars, 21 video testimonials on the homepage, Shark Tank credibility, press from FOX, NBC, USA Today, GQ. You've earned trust that money can't buy. The "skeptic to obsessed in one rally" conversion narrative is clever — it names the exact objection ("This looks dumb") and walks the visitor through the journey of overcoming it.

**Tom's story is your superpower.** The Our Story page — the sobriety narrative, how Pepper Pong was born from recovery, Rally 4 Recovery — that's the kind of founder story that earns press coverage, customer loyalty, and word-of-mouth. You can't manufacture authenticity like that.

**Your Schools page is the best page on the site.** Clear value props, SEL research references, educator-specific messaging, volume pricing. It's a model for how every page on the site should work. (More on why this matters in a minute.)

## The Big Opportunity

Here's the single most important thing in this audit:

**Pepper Pong converts great. The problem is discovery.**

If someone searches "Pepper Pong" — you own the entire first page of Google. Branded search is completely locked down. Anyone who already knows the name can find you and buy.

But if someone searches "mini pickleball game," "best tabletop games for families," "fun office break room games," or "PE games for schools" — you're invisible. These are the searches from people who would *love* Pepper Pong but don't know it exists yet.

We researched 10 keywords a potential customer might search. **You're visible for 4 and invisible for 6.** The invisible ones are the discovery terms — the searches that bring in new customers who've never heard of Pepper Pong.

**Why?** There's a vocabulary mismatch. Your site calls Pepper Pong a "portable paddle sport." Searchers are looking for "mini pickleball set," "tabletop game," and "indoor ping pong." Google can't connect the dots because your site never uses the words people actually type.

This is fixable. And it doesn't require a redesign — it requires content and copy changes.

## The Shark Tank Clock Is Ticking

Google Trends shows search interest in Pepper Pong peaked in February 2026 (Shark Tank replay bump) and has dropped from 100 to 8-20 by April. Interest spikes around events — Shark Tank airings, holiday gift season — but there's nothing sustaining it between events.

Without new content driving organic discovery, search interest will keep decaying. The Shark Tank wave brought attention. Content marketing is what turns that attention into a permanent audience.

The blog (5 posts, last one 15 months ago) is the biggest missed opportunity on the site. Every competitor ranking above Pepper Pong on discovery keywords has active content. You have unlimited material — Shark Tank, 100K+ sales, schools program, Rally 4 Recovery, 15+ use cases — and zero published content about any of it.

## Five Things to Fix This Week

These are high-impact, low-effort. Under an hour each.

### 1. Write Meta Descriptions for Your Key Pages

18 of your 20 content pages (How to Play, Our Story, Reviews, Press, FAQ, and more) have no meta description. That means Google auto-generates the snippet it shows in search results — and it's usually not great.

Your homepage and products already have them (good). The rest need them. Here are drafts for the five most important:

- **Our Story:** "The story behind Pepper Pong: how Tom's journey through recovery led to the game 100,000+ people can't put down."
- **How to Play:** "Learn Pepper Pong in 60 seconds. Watch the quick start video, download the rulebook, and turn any table into your center court."
- **FAQ:** "Everything you need to know about Pepper Pong — how it compares to ping pong, what's in the set, the three ball types, shipping, and returns."
- **Press:** "Pepper Pong in the news: Shark Tank Season 16, FOX, NBC, USA Today, GQ. The game they're calling 'the next pickleball.'"
- **Reviews:** "211+ five-star reviews from families, educators, and offices. See why players go from skeptic to obsessed in one rally."

In Shopify, these are in the SEO section at the bottom of each page editor. 5 minutes per page.

### 2. Remove Your Draft and Internal Pages from Google

Three pages are publicly visible and indexed in your sitemap that shouldn't be:
- `/pages/pdp-draft` — literally titled "PDP Draft"
- `/pages/email-confirmation` — an internal confirmation page
- `/pages/lp1` — a test landing page with an opaque slug

Set these to draft in Shopify or add noindex tags. They're minor, but they make the site look unpolished to anyone (or any search engine) that finds them.

### 3. Secure Your NIRSA Discount Codes

Your `/pages/nirsa` page displays two active discount codes in plain text — `1SAMPLENIRSA26` (61% off) and `8PACKNIRSA26` (62% off). The page is publicly indexed. Anyone can find and use these codes until they expire April 30.

If these are meant for NIRSA attendees only, gate the page with a password, use Shopify's automatic discounts with customer tags, or at minimum noindex the page.

### 4. Add FAQ Schema to Your FAQ Page

Your FAQ page has 15+ well-written Q&As — and several match exactly what people search on Google ("Is Pepper Pong worth it?", "Why is Pepper Pong so expensive?", "Is it sold in stores?"). Adding FAQ structured data means these answers can appear directly in Google search results as expandable rich snippets. Free real estate. A Shopify schema app handles this in 15-30 minutes.

### 5. Add Schools to Your Navigation

Your Schools page is the best-structured content page on the entire site. It sells a $599 product. And it's not in the main navigation — an educator would never find it unless they had the direct URL. Add "Schools" or "Educators" to the nav. One link. Huge impact for a revenue channel you've clearly invested in.

## The Revenue Channels You're Missing

### The Education Market Is Dark

You have school-specific products (8-Pack PLUS at $599), a PE Playbook, curriculum PDFs, SHAPE and NIRSA editions, and a standards alignment document. You've clearly invested in this market. But when PE teachers search "PE games for schools indoor," they find WeAreTeachers, Gopher PE Blog, and PEGames.org. They don't find you.

The Schools page needs a meta description, a place in the main nav, and content that uses the search terms educators actually type — "indoor PE games," "school sports equipment," "social-emotional learning activities."

### The Corporate Market Doesn't Know You Exist

"Indoor game for office" has 10 Google Autocomplete suggestions — people search for break room games, team building games, Fun Friday ideas, holiday party activities. Pepper Pong is a perfect fit (quiet, portable, any table, 60-second setup), but there's zero content targeting this audience. A single landing page — "Pepper Pong for the Office" — could open an entirely new buyer segment.

### The Search Language Gap

"Mini pickleball" gets 10 autocomplete suggestions — a full, active search category (courts, sets, paddles, nets). Your product fits this category, but the site never uses the phrase. People searching "mini pickleball set" or "mini pickleball game" would be your customers — if they could find you.

Adding "mini pickleball," "tabletop game," and "indoor ping pong" language to your product pages and homepage is the single highest-impact SEO change available.

## Where AI Fits In

You don't need a bigger team. You need a smarter workflow. Here's where AI tools make the biggest difference for a brand at your stage:

**Content that drives discovery.** The #1 finding in this audit is the gap between people who search and people who find you. AI can produce targeted blog posts and landing pages — "Best Tabletop Games for Family Game Night," "5 Break Room Games That Get People Talking," "Why PE Teachers Are Adding Pepper Pong" — at a pace a small team couldn't sustain manually. One post per week, targeting one non-branded keyword, can meaningfully move search visibility in 3-6 months.

**Consistent product descriptions.** Your Full Set product page is thorough. Not every product matches that quality. AI can draft descriptions from specs and brand voice guidelines, then Tom reviews. Every product gets the same depth.

**Email sequences.** Klaviyo is already installed. AI can help build post-purchase flows (setup guide → UGC request → review ask → referral), abandoned cart sequences that address the price objection, and education-specific nurtures.

**Social proof at scale.** 211+ reviews and 21 video testimonials, but the content isn't organized or leveraged. AI can extract the best quotes by buyer type (families, educators, offices), generate social posts from reviews, and turn press mentions into shareable assets.

## The Housekeeping

A few smaller items worth addressing:

- **Social media links are buried.** Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok icons exist — but they're tiny, in the sub-footer copyright bar. For a product that thrives on video content, social links should be prominent.
- **Footer "SHOP" link goes to one product** (`/products/pepper-pong-full-set`) instead of the full catalog. A customer looking for replacement balls or bundles has to hunt.
- **"Read our FAQ's"** in the footer — should be "FAQs" (no apostrophe).
- **OG image uses HTTP instead of HTTPS.** When shared on social media, some platforms flag mixed content. Quick fix in theme settings.
- **No Twitter/X card image.** Sharing the URL on X shows no image or a random one. Add a `twitter:image` meta tag.
- **Product title inconsistencies** — `spicy-solo` slug appears as the display title, mixed casing on "Extra" prefix across products.
- **No announcement bar.** You have active promotions (NIRSA deal, bundle discounts, free shipping) but no homepage banner surfacing them.
- **Our Story page undertells the story.** The recovery narrative is powerful, but there's no Shark Tank chapter, no growth milestones, no "garage game to 100,000 tables" arc. This page could be twice as compelling with the full journey.
- **Blog post titles need work.** "Blog 1: Backstory" and "Tom's New Blog Post" don't help SEO or brand credibility.
- **No Google Analytics or Facebook Pixel.** If you're running ads anywhere, you can't track conversions back to the site.
- **Image alt text needs attention.** 8 images use "Press logo" (should name the publication), 6 use "Thumbnail 1-6" (should describe the image), 5 have empty alt text. This affects both SEO and screen reader accessibility.
- **Collection naming.** "core-products" is titled "Extra Peppers" — misleading. "frontpage" is titled "Home page" — confusing.
- **No Organization schema.** Adding structured data with business name, logo, and social profiles helps Google build a Knowledge Panel — achievable for a brand with Shark Tank press.
