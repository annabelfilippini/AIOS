# Reference Sites — Hop Alley
Chosen 2026-04-14/15. Scraped to `reference/{atomix,cosme,fatcow,mister-jius}/` during original run. Breadcrumb written 2026-04-16 (post-ship, pre-cleanup) from `scrape-data.md` lines 160–175 + each reference's own `notes.md` where present.

## Mister Jiu's — https://misterjius.com
Why chosen: Direct parallel — Michelin-starred Chinese restaurant, SF. Closest peer in category, cuisine, and award tier.
Execution takeaways for Hop Alley:
- Michelin star rendered as iconography above the fold, not a text string. Ships as schema markup + visual badge. Directly fixes Hop Alley's "awards are invisible to Google" finding.
- `/reservations` lives on-domain with Tock embedded, not as an external bounce to exploretock.com. Framed as "Book" not "Reservations →".
- Menu is HTML, not PDF. Indexable, mobile-readable, no Canva middleman. This is the single biggest UX fix for Hop Alley's five-PDF menu problem.
- Chef story page: Brandon Jew bio, origin narrative, press quotes. Tommy Lee has equivalent or better credentials (Michelin, JBF semifinalist, 5280) with zero on-site presence.
- Typography: serif display + sans body. Editorial density without clutter.
Do NOT copy: Cantonese-California heritage palette (warm golds, traditional motifs). Hop Alley is Northern Chinese — identity stays its own.

## Atomix — https://atomixnyc.com
Why chosen: Michelin 2-star, Korean fine dining. Aspirational-peer for award-forward minimalism and chef-as-protagonist narrative.
Execution takeaways for Hop Alley:
- Homepage is radically minimal — logo, one sentence, nav. Confidence through restraint.
- About page carries the weight: chef bio (Junghyun Park), brand philosophy, press-logo grid. Michelin ⭐⭐ treated as art, not footnote.
- Same Tock backend as Hop Alley, but surfaced as "Book" — reframing, not re-platforming.
Do NOT copy: Atomix's ultra-austere monochrome posture. Hop Alley is a Denver neighborhood restaurant with a Beijing-alley identity — magenta accent and Rino-neighborhood energy stay. Borrow the restraint and the press-grid move, not the austerity.

## Cosme — https://cosmenyc.com
Why chosen: Enrique Olvera's NYC flagship. One-page narrative and inline-menu execution — the pattern that kills Hop Alley's Canva-PDF problem without introducing a CMS rebuild.
Execution takeaways for Hop Alley:
- Single saturated ground (oxblood) carries the entire identity — no chrome fighting the content. Hop Alley could pick one committed ground (dark green, maroon, charred brown) instead of default-black with magenta accent.
- Vertical wordmark as architecture: "COSME" / "DAMIAN" set vertical on page edges reads as signage, not logo placement. "HOP ALLEY" on-edge is an obvious carry-over.
- One-page narrative: hero → identity paragraph → team portraits (name + role, no bios) → menu as styled text → gallery → short footer. No menu PDF, no external bounces.
- Menu rendered inline as body text (dish name bold, description lowercase, price at end). Dietary tags per line. Direct template for the HTML-menu fix.
- Team cards quietly: three square portraits, name + role, no bios. Good model for surfacing Tommy Lee and Doug Rankin without chef-bio prose.
- Two-face typography max: heavy condensed serif + humanist sans. Restraint is the move.
Do NOT copy: the oxblood ground itself (Cosme's signature). Mexican fine-dining posture. Hop Alley's magenta accent stays load-bearing — single-color grounds should be tested in Hop Alley's own palette, not Cosme's.

## Fatcow — https://fatcow.com.sg
Why chosen: Dark-ground editorial pattern where food photography is the only saturated color source. Benchmark for "how to do dark without going goth."
Execution takeaways for Hop Alley:
- Hero: single oversized top-down dish shot on near-black. Restaurant name sits small and centered above. No headline competes with the photograph — food is the headline.
- Color discipline: near-black ground, warm reds/charred browns, muted-gold accent used sparingly. Chrome never competes with food color. Applied to Hop Alley: dark ground, food as only saturated color source, magenta moved to a single accent use.
- Disciplined 3-up promo grid (omakase / wine dinner / special event) with equal-width full-bleed photos and small-caps titles. Exact pattern for Hop Alley's Chef's Counter / Happy Hour / Large Party tiles, replacing today's stacked text links.
- Instagram embed row as native Meta cards (not thumbnails). Proves the account is active without stealing tone.
- Thin display serif + plain sans. Quietness is the luxury signal.
- Menu page: clean table, bold dish name, lighter description, right-aligned price, no photos on the menu page itself (photos live on the homepage).
Do NOT copy: the muted-gold accent specifically (Fatcow's signature) or the Japanese-wagyu-atelier positioning. Hop Alley's magenta accent stays. Borrow the dark-ground discipline and the food-as-only-color rule.

## Guiding principle (carry forward to any future re-run)
References inform execution quality — award iconography, on-domain reservations, HTML menus, dark-ground discipline, editorial typography pairings, one-page narrative architecture.
Identity stays Hop Alley's own: Tommy Lee's Michelin/JBF narrative, Northern Chinese cuisine, Rino neighborhood energy, magenta accent, Beijing-alley name story. Per `feedback_elevate_not_erase` — elevate, don't erase.
