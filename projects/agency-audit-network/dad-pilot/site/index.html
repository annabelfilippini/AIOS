Why this isn't ChatGPT

Closed loops, not open loops

A thermostat measures the room and adjusts. Most AI workflows are heaters. We build thermostats. Three loops, nested.

Three nested loops

Inner · days

Did this draft work?

Email sent vs draft (what got edited?). Reply received? LinkedIn engagement logged. Next draft reads what worked.

Middle · weeks

Are patterns sharpening?

Weekly cron surfaces top objections, themes without skills, trends. Erica updates rules or promotes a new skill.

Outer · months

Is the voice sharper?

Monthly diff proposed against voice / ICP / offer. System proposes. Humans approve.

What it reads before drafting

Input 1

voice.md (community)

Warm, peer-to-peer. Allows "stoked," "friends," "come find us." Anchored in aviation outcomes. No insurance language, no "luxury redefined."

Input 2

Top posts (90 days)

Sorted by engagement. Seed pool: Tuvoli, NBAA shuttle seats, Charter Connect recap, office dog Ace, Josh's $4M / 10% AOG stats.

Input 3

This week's call themes

Top 5 recurring themes from Granola. If topic is blank, the skill proposes three directions.

The skill

Lives at skills/cfs-linkedin-post/SKILL.md. Drops in on day one.

---
name: cfs-linkedin-post
description: Draft a LinkedIn post in CFS voice. Reads voice file,
  recent high-engagement posts, and this-week Granola themes.
inputs:
  - topic (string, required)
  - register (community | thought_leadership, default: community)
  - length_target (short 60-90 | medium 90-150, default: medium)
context_files:
  - voice.md
context_queries:
  - supabase: SELECT post_text, impressions, reactions
    FROM linkedin_posts
    WHERE posted_at > now() - interval '90 days'
    ORDER BY (reactions::float / NULLIF(impressions, 0)) DESC
    LIMIT 10
  - supabase: SELECT theme_tag, count(*) FROM calls
    WHERE ingested_at > now() - interval '7 days'
    GROUP BY theme_tag ORDER BY count DESC LIMIT 5
---

# Rubric — every check must pass

 1. Length: 60-150 words (or 60-90 short).
 2. Register: community by default.
 3. Names a specific aviation moment.
 4. Uses load-bearing vocab: AOG, mechanical, recovery, broker,
    operator, replacement aircraft, cost difference, Part 135.
 5. No forbidden phrases: "luxury redefined," "elevate,"
    "white glove," "game-changing," "frictionless," "unlock,"
    "disrupt," "revolutionary," "never worry," "risk-free."
 6. No insurance language.
 7. No safety or operational-control claims.
 8. Ends with practical implication or quiet invitation.
 9. Peer-to-peer, not corporate.
10. Pricing / eligibility / reimbursement → human template.

Charter Connect recap community · medium · 117w
Great few days at Charter Connect. No booth this year, just good conversations. The most useful ones weren't about features or pricing. They were about the actual decisions brokers and operators have to make when an aircraft goes mechanical: which replacement option protects the client experience, what the cost delta does to the trip economics, and how you keep your team focused on client communication while recovery logistics happen behind the scenes. Those decisions don't change much from year to year. The way teams prepare for them does. Thanks to everyone who made time to sit down with us — and to Goodwin for co-hosting an event that keeps getting the format right.

Passes: length · community register · event moment · vocab · partner-thanks ending

The $30K decision thought leadership · 130w
A mechanical event isn't one decision. It's two stacked on top of each other. Decision one is operational: where's the recovery aircraft, what's its tail history, who's the operator, is it on the DNU list. Decision two is financial: what does the cost delta do to the trip — and to the relationship. When the on-time replacement costs $30,000 more, most brokers don't get to choose freely. The math forces a tradeoff between client experience and trip economics. That tradeoff is where a lot of broker-operator relationships quietly erode — not because anyone made the wrong call, but because someone had to absorb a loss that wasn't theirs to absorb. The recovery decision is operational. The cost decision is structural. They're worth separating.

Passes: length · thought-leadership register · specific tension · vocab · practical implication ending

Built to grow

How this scales past Tom

Five additions that keep the system healthy past month twelve, scale to Erica and future hires without a rebuild, and let new skills get added without Annabel writing every one.

shared brain scoped per seat checked before you see it evolves over time

Patch 1 · multi-seat

One brain. Each seat sees only what they should.

Supabase is the company's shared brain — every seat reads from and writes to it. Row-Level Security (a Postgres feature) makes the database itself enforce who sees what, so a forgotten filter in a worker can't leak Tom's queue to Erica.

RLS on from day one. The data shape is multi-tenant before the second user exists — so adding Erica is a config change, not a migration.

Why now, not at Phase 5. Turning RLS on later with three seats already querying means rewriting every worker. Doing it before the second seat exists costs nothing and locks in the design.

Patch 2 · clean scaling

Add a venture, not new code.

Every ingestion worker is one template parameterized by {source, venture, auth_method}. Adding the 8th system in month 6 is a config row, not a new worker.

The template costs an extra two hours in Phase 1. Saves two weeks at Phase 4 and every Phase after.

Patch 3 · quality gate

Drafts get checked before you see them.

The drafter doesn't put a draft into your queue directly. A second pass — the validator — checks the draft against a fixed rubric. Pass → your queue. Fail → Annabel's review queue. Hallucinations and shape errors never reach Tom as a "first draft to fix."

The rubric tightens at every L2 review — false-positive checks get relaxed, hallucinations that slip through get a new check added.

Patch 4 · longevity

The system tells you when it's drifting.

Every Sunday at 22:00 MT, a worker checks six things that quietly break AI systems over time. Any flag emails Annabel that night and shows up in Tom's Friday message.

Mansel's quarterly health check, weekly instead. If a flag goes unresolved 2 weeks running, it blocks the next phase shipping.

Patch 5 · evolves over time

Erica adds her own skills.

A skill is one row in loops + a drafter prompt + a validator rubric + an L1 design. Erica writes the spec, Annabel reviews feasibility, Tom approves, Annabel scaffolds, ships heater first. Same closed-loops rule as Tom's workflows.

Erica's likely first skills: content_draft and email_draft — both reuse the existing drafter shape. She proposes them via the spec template the day she gets her Cowork seat.
