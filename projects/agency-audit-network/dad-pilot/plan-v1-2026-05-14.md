## The one-paragraph summary

We're going to install an AI operating system at your company, one person at a time, starting with Erica (marketing/sales). Erica gets a dedicated AI on her laptop that already knows your company's voice, ICP, and offer — so it drafts emails, LinkedIn posts, and newsletters in *your* tone, not generic AI tone. Every client call she records gets automatically synthesized and stored in a shared company brain so the next hire inherits everything Erica's learned. Estimated stack cost: ~$150–300/month. Estimated time to "Erica's life is meaningfully different": 4 weeks. The first employee is the proof — once it works for Erica, the same setup gets cloned for hire #2, hire #3, with each new person plugging into the same shared brain.

---

## The four ideas this is built on

These come from two AI consultants Annabel researched in depth (Mansel Scheffel and Bo Sar). Their frameworks converge on four principles:

1. **The "Federated Personal, Shared Backbone" architecture.** Each employee has their own private AI setup (personal skills, personal memory) but reads from a shared company brain (your voice, your offer, your clients). When a new hire joins, they plug into the brain and inherit institutional knowledge instantly.
2. **One pod at a time.** Don't try to AI-ify the whole company at once. Pick one person, one workflow, prove it, then expand. Mansel's exact warning: *"don't Gestapo the whole company at once."*
3. **Define what good looks like, then let AI hit the bar.** Before building any skill, write down the rubric (e.g., "a good outbound email is 80-120 words, names a specific outcome, has one CTA"). The AI self-checks against the rubric. This is the difference between AI drafts you delete and AI drafts you send.
4. **Closed loops only.** Every workflow has a feedback mechanism. Newsletter sent → open rates flow back. Client call recorded → analyzed → feeds next call prep. No open-ended "fire and forget."

**How to read this:**
- **Erica's laptop** is the only place she touches. She uses Cowork like a chat app.
- **Dropbox** holds 5 markdown files that define your company's voice, customer, offer, and goals. Everyone's AI reads from these.
- **Cloudflare Workers** is the always-on plumbing that pulls data from external services (Granola, Gmail, Beehiiv) on a schedule and writes it to Supabase.
- **Supabase** is the company-wide database. When you hire someone tomorrow, they plug into the same Supabase and inherit everything.

## What Erica's day actually looks like (after 4 weeks)

**7:00 AM** — Daily Brief email arrives in her inbox. 5 minutes to read. Synthesizes:
- New leads from yesterday's calls (auto-pulled from Granola)
- Replies needed (auto-pulled from Gmail, prioritized by deal stage)
- Newsletter performance (auto-pulled from Beehiiv)
- Competitor mentions (auto-pulled from web monitoring)

**9:00 AM** — She has a call with a prospect. Granola records. After the call, she goes to lunch. Cloudflare worker pulls the transcript automatically. By the time she's back, Supabase has the call logged, a draft follow-up email is sitting in her Gmail drafts, and the company brain has learned which objections that prospect raised.

**11:00 AM** — She opens Cowork and types `/draft linkedin post about our new offer`. Cowork reads your voice file, your offer file, and three of your best past LinkedIn posts (stored in Supabase). Drafts a post in your voice. She reviews, edits 2 sentences, schedules.

**2:00 PM** — Newsletter cron job fires on its scheduled day. Cowork drafts the newsletter from this week's wins and metrics, sends it to Erica for approval. She reviews, clicks send. Beehiiv API ships it. Open rates flow back to Supabase by next week.

**5:00 PM** — She closes her laptop. The system keeps working: Cloudflare pulls overnight data, Supabase logs it, tomorrow's Daily Brief starts assembling.

## What we're building, in order

The order matters. Each skill builds on the one before it.

### Week 1 — Foundation (no skills yet, just the brain)
- Set up Supabase project + initial schema
- Set up Cloudflare account
- Set up Cowork on Erica's laptop
- **The five Dropbox markdown files** (`my-voice.md`, `my-icp.md`, `my-business.md`, `offer.md`, `goals.md`)
- *Annabel drafts these with you (dad). They define everything downstream. This is the highest-leverage hour we spend.*

### Week 2 — Skill #1: Daily Brief
- The wedge skill. Both Mansel and Bo say to build this first.
- Pulls Granola transcripts from yesterday, Gmail unread count, Beehiiv stats, competitor signals
- Delivers as a 7am email to Erica
- *Why first:* it forces the entire pipeline (Cloudflare + Supabase + external APIs) to work end-to-end. If Daily Brief works, every other skill is easier.

### Week 3 — Skill #2: Email writer
- Highest daily-volume skill — biggest immediate time saver for Erica
- Reads the incoming Gmail thread, drafts a reply that meets the test harness (length, tone, CTA, signature)
- *Drafts only, never auto-sends.* Erica reviews every draft.

### Week 4 — Skill #3: LinkedIn writer
- `/draft linkedin post about [topic]` in Cowork
- Reads voice file + recent posts from Supabase
- Outputs a post + 3 variations
- Test harness rubric ensures consistent tone

### Week 5 — Skill #4: Newsletter writer + cron
- Weekly Beehiiv newsletter
- Drafts on Mondays for Wednesday send
- Cloudflare cron fires the draft
- Erica approves before Beehiiv API sends

### Week 6 — Closed-loop the newsletter
- Beehiiv → Cloudflare → Supabase: open rates, click rates, unsubscribes
- Next newsletter reads last 4 weeks of performance before drafting
- Now it's a *closed* loop. Bo's principle.

### Week 7+ — Employee #2
- Same architecture, new person plugs in
- Inherits everything Erica's AI has learned

## Roles (Bo's framework, applied)

Bo names three roles in an AI-first company. They map cleanly here:

| Role | Person | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **AI Founder** | Dad | "You cannot outsource your conviction on the power of these tools." Doesn't have to code, but has to understand what the system does and why it matters. |
| **DRI (Directly Responsible Individual)** | Erica | Owns a *result*, not a task list. Her DRI assignment: "Lead pipeline + brand voice." She's accountable for the outcome — not for which AI skill drafts which email. |
| **Builder** | Annabel | Builds the skills, wires the pipeline, fixes what breaks. Eventually transfers ownership to Dad's team. |

This matters because **the goal is not "Erica uses AI for tasks."** The goal is "Erica owns the lead pipeline outcome and uses AI as her team."

## What Dad doesn't need to worry about

- **Erica being replaced by AI.** Skills draft, Erica approves. Nothing auto-sends to clients without her looking at it. Her job becomes *higher-judgment*, not eliminated.
- **Data security.** Credentials stay with the company. API keys live in a password manager. Database backups are automatic in Supabase. Nothing in this stack is doing anything sensitive that your existing tools aren't already doing.
- **Vendor lock-in.** Everything is markdown files + Postgres + standard APIs. If we ever swap Cowork for a different tool, the skills and context move with us.
- **Maintenance burden falling on you.** Annabel owns this until it's stable. Eventually we may train someone on your team to own it, but only when it's mature.

## What could go wrong (the honest list)

Mansel and Bo both flag these failure modes. We're naming them so they're not surprises.

1. **Silent failures.** OAuth tokens expire, model behavior drifts, context goes stale. Mansel's fix: run a quarterly "health check" — fresh-environment restore to confirm everything still works. We'll build this in week 3.
2. **The five Dropbox files are vague.** If `my-voice.md` is generic, every email draft is generic. We mitigate by spending real time on these *first*.
3. **Erica doesn't adopt it.** Bo's warning: the employee has to develop their own conviction. If Erica thinks of it as "Annabel's project," it fails. Mitigation: Erica should pair with Annabel on building her own first skill, not have it handed to her.
4. **We automate a broken process.** Mansel's rule: *"Automating an efficient 3-step process is cheaper and more performant than automating a bloated 7-step mess."* Before each skill we ask: is this process actually good? Can we delete steps before adding AI? This is built into the build sequence.
5. **Scope creep into Employee #2 too fast.** We don't onboard a second person until Erica's setup has been running for 2+ weeks with no fires.
