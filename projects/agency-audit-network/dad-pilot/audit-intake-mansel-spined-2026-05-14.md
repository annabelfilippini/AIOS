## The framework spine (what Mansel taught us to look for)

The audit isn't a custom shape invented for you — it applies Mansel Scheffel's AIOS audit framework (the one I built the agency-audit-network around) to your specific entity. Five elements from Mansel and Bo, applied verbatim:

1. **6-Layer Business Brain (Bo Sar).** Identity / Critical context / Working memory / Episodic memory / Long-term knowledge / Background decay. Each scored 0–5. Your voice / ICP / offer canon files fall inside Layers 1–2. Scored once per company.

2. **4 Pods (Mansel).** Acquisition / Delivery / Support / Operations. The audit identifies which pods are active in each company and where the friction lives. (Mansel uses *pods* in his current curriculum, not *engines*.)

3. **Friction Tags (Mansel + Bo).** 🟡 Time Sink, 🟠 Wait / Handoff, 🔴 Quality Risk, 🟣 Compliance Risk, 🔁 Open Loop. Applied to every workflow step the audit identifies.

4. **QDOAA sequencing (Mansel).** Question → Delete → Optimize → Accelerate → Automate. The non-obvious rule: AI gets added last, after the broken process is fixed. Mansel's literal claim — most processes have 30–40% of steps that can be cut without AI at all.

5. **Opportunity Matrix + Money Slide (Mansel).** Quick Wins scored by `(Impact × Confidence) − (Effort + Risk + (6 − Adoption) + (6 − Data Readiness))`. Each scored Quick Win gets a Cost-of-Inaction dollar number. This is what makes the pilot pick non-arbitrary.

Why these five: they're what Mansel charges $10K–$70K to produce for clients. The audit's job is to produce them for you against your existing materials in ~2 days of compute time.

## What the audit does autonomously (zero work from you)

The audit applies Mansel's framework against your existing materials. Two passes:

### Light pass — across all 4 companies (AIH, CFS, Falcon, FSL)

- **6-Layer Business Brain scoring.** For each company, scan the vault, website, LinkedIn, and channels. Score each of the 6 layers 0–5 with evidence quotes from your materials. Output: a radar chart per company.
- **Pod inventory.** Identify which of Mansel's 4 pods (Acquisition / Delivery / Support / Operations) are active in each company. Map the materials you already have to each pod.
- **Friction signals.** Scan Granola transcripts, sent emails, public testimonials, and case studies for explicit complaints, wait times, rework mentions, open-loop handoffs. Tag what's found with Mansel's 🟡🟠🔴🟣🔁.
- **AIOS-level recommendation per company.** Pick one of Mansel's four headline recommendations: **Personal AIOS / Team AIOS / Company AIOS / Cleanup-before-automation.** Honest call about whether each company is ready for AI or needs process redesign first.
- **System inventory across the entity.** Existing tools, plan tiers, what's missing for the target architecture, what's misconfigured.

### Deep pass — pilot candidate only (likely CFS or Falcon)

- **One workflow mapped with Mansel's Step Cards.** Per step: owner, systems, inputs / outputs, volume, doing time, wait time, error rate, SLA, data sensitivity, controls, DRI, open-loop check.
- **Friction tags applied** at the step level.
- **QDOAA pass.** Walk the workflow through Question → Delete → Optimize → Accelerate → Automate, in that order. Surface which steps shouldn't exist before recommending what to automate.
- **Opportunity Matrix.** Score every flagged step. Pull Quick Wins (low effort × high impact). Plot on the 2×2.
- **Money Slide.** Cost-of-Inaction calculation for the top 3 Quick Wins. Annual time saved + revenue uplift + risk avoided. Payback in months.

The audit comes back with **Mansel's 7 discovery questions** per company (the only stuff it genuinely cannot infer):

1. *"Walk me through this company's main process end-to-end. Where does it slow down or break?"*

4. *"When it fails, what happens? Lost revenue? Refunds? Rework? Compliance risk?"*

6. *"If we fixed it, what improves: speed, accuracy, capacity, conversion, risk?"*
7. *"What would solving this be worth this year if it worked?"*

Plus 1–2 company-specific gap questions the audit will surface (goals for the next 6–12 months, ICP exclusions, anything its 6-Layer Brain scan flagged with low confidence).

## Pilot recommendation logic

The audit ranks the four companies using **Mansel's priority formula** applied across all the workflows it finds:

```
Priority = (Impact × Confidence) − (Effort + Risk + (6 − Adoption) + (6 − Data Readiness))
```

Applied to your entity:

1. **Impact** — Cost-of-Inaction dollar number for the company's biggest Quick Win (Money Slide output).
2. **Confidence** — how much data the audit had to work with. Companies with more Granola transcripts, more vault material, more website content score higher.
3. **Effort** — how much new infrastructure each company needs vs. what's already in place.
4. **Risk** — friction signals around compliance, data sensitivity, customer-facing failure modes.
5. **Adoption** — how ready the people involved are. Mostly relevant for whoever's running the pod day-to-day.
6. **Data Readiness** — Mansel's question: is the data clean, accessible, permissioned?

Highest score wins the pilot. The audit shows you the score breakdown — push back if your instinct says different. The reason this matters: "which company has more material" picks the easiest one; **Mansel's formula picks the one with the biggest dollar-backed Quick Win available**, which is the one that pays back the setup investment fastest.

## Editing the canon after it's locked

The normalized canon (`voice.md`, `icp.md`, `offer.md`, `business.md`, `goals.md` — which feed Layers 1–2 of the 6-Layer Business Brain) lives in your Obsidian vault by default. You're fine in Obsidian. Josh isn't — and the employees coming after him probably won't be either. So the canon needs an editing surface that works for someone who doesn't live in markdown.

The audit will recommend one of three options and land the choice in the setup checklist:

1. **Guided Obsidian, simplified view.** Obsidian's Live Preview mode is close to WYSIWYG. Josh gets a vault with only the canon folder visible, no plugins, no folder maze. Cheapest, no new tools — but he still has to open Obsidian.
2. **Notion (or similar) as the front door.** Canon files mirrored into Notion; edits sync back to the vault. Most familiar surface for a non-technical editor. Adds one tool to the stack.
3. **Per-file shared docs (Google Docs / similar).** Each canon file is a shared doc; edits sync back. Simplest mental model, most fragile sync pipe.

The audit recommends one based on what Josh already uses day-to-day; you and Annabel land the final call on the review screenshare. Whichever wins is the same surface every future employee edits through — so this is one decision, not a repeated one.

## What the audit explicitly will not do

- **Will not modify your originals.** Mansel-shaped outputs land in new files; your vault notes are untouched.
- **Will not auto-decide anything.** Pilot recommendation, AIOS-level call per company, gap fills, setup choices — all proposed, all reviewable, all your call.
- **Will not scan anything you flagged as excluded.** Default is the whole vault; tell us what to skip.
- **Will not store your raw materials outside the systems they already live in.** Extractions land in your vault. Nothing ships to anyone.
- **Will not spin up paid services without your approval.** Setup checklist is a plan; spend is your call.
- **Will not recommend automating something Mansel's QDOAA pass says should be deleted, optimized, or accelerated first.** Process cleanup wins before AI gets layered in. This is the single most-violated rule in AI consulting and the reason most pilots fail; the audit refuses to skip it.
