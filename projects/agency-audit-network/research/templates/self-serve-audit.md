## Section 1 — What this is + what you'll get

**What this is.** A guided diagnostic that turns the messy reality of your business into a structured brief an AI agency can act on.

**What you'll fill out.** A short business snapshot, one workflow you want to fix, the people / tools / steps inside it, the friction, the dollars at stake, and the 2–3 fixes you think come first.

**What you'll get back.** A PDF with:
- A one-page business snapshot.
- Your chosen workflow mapped, tagged, and scored.
- A prioritized list of fixes with numeric Definitions of Done.
- An ROI + Cost of Inaction section showing dollars saved / unlocked per fix.
- An **Agency Handoff Brief** — the page you give to an agency so they can quote and build without re-interviewing you.

**Time to complete.** ~60–90 minutes. You can save and resume.

**What the audit will not do.** It will not build the fix. It will not pick the agency for you (unless you ask Annabel to at the very end). It will not tell you to automate a broken process — it'll flag if your process needs cleanup before automation makes sense.

## Section 2 — Your business at a glance (the 6-Layer Brain)

Before any workflow, score the brain. AI doesn't fix broken processes — it amplifies them. If your business knowledge lives in your head and walks out the door when someone quits, every automation we recommend will sit on quicksand.

Score each layer **0–5**.

| Layer | What we mean | Score |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Identity** | Mission, products, ICP, brand voice, founder POV — written down somewhere stable. | ☐ 0–5 |
| **2. Critical context** | This quarter's goals, active campaigns, current pricing, deals in flight. | ☐ 0–5 |
| **3. Working memory** | This week's sprint, in-progress deals, this week's content. | ☐ 0–5 |
| **4. Episodic memory** | *Why* decisions were made — paused outbound, fired a vendor, pivoted a segment. | ☐ 0–5 |
| **5. Long-term knowledge** | Playbooks, won / lost notes, case studies, customer interviews. | ☐ 0–5 |
| **6. Background decay** | A process that promotes current info and decays stale info. | ☐ 0–5 |

**How to score.**

- **0** — does not exist
- **1** — exists in someone's head only
- **2** — exists somewhere but no one knows where
- **3** — documented but not discoverable / not maintained
- **4** — documented, discoverable, occasionally updated
- **5** — documented, discoverable, automatically maintained, machine-readable

**What your average tells you.**

- **Average 0–1.5** — your brain is empty. Headline recommendation: *Cleanup before automation.* Spend the next 90 days writing things down, not buying tools.
- **Average 1.5–2.5** — partial brain, typical small business. You can automate one workflow now; you'll need to fill more of the brain before scaling.
- **Average 2.5–4** — strong foundation. You're ready for multiple automations; pick the one with the biggest dollar number.
- **Average 4+** — rare. The audit will be mostly about marginal improvements and Big Swings.

> Source: Bo Sar. This is the one Bo piece used up front because it's diagnostic and easy to self-score.

## Section 4 — Tell us about your role and your team (Leadership view)

Answer as the person who owns the KPI. If you *are* the owner-operator, you answer for yourself.

> Source: Mansel, Phase 1 — Stakeholder Interviews. Questions preserved verbatim, framing changed from "interviewer asks" to "you answer."

### 1. Role & team
- What's your role, and what's your team responsible for?
- What are the main KPIs your team owns this quarter / this year?
- Who owns the workflow you picked, and who is accountable if it improves or breaks?

### 2. Core processes & workflow
- Where are the biggest bottlenecks, delays, or expensive handoffs in this workflow?
- Which tasks consume the most hours, dollars, or leadership attention?

### 3. Tools & technology
- What are the main tools your team relies on, and where do people work outside the official system?

### 4. Pain points & strategic challenges
- If you could fix one workflow in the next 90 days, what would move the business most? Why?
- What prevents the team from being more efficient or effective today?

### 5. Future vision
- Where are the biggest opportunities for improvement in the next 6–12 months?

### 6. Cultural & change management
- Who would champion a new tool, who would resist, and who needs to sign off? Name roles or people if you can.

### Add-ons — tie pain to dollars
- **KPI under pressure.** Which KPI is most at risk if this workflow stays the same? What happens financially if you miss?
- **Cost of pain.** Roughly how many people / hours are tied up in your top 2 bottlenecks?
- **Risk lens.** Any compliance, security, or customer risks tied to current processes?

Now switch perspectives. Answer as the person who *does* the work — the SDR, the AM, the analyst, the AP clerk, the support agent, the coordinator. If you do the work yourself, answer for yourself. If your employee does, fill it out *as* them — or better, have them fill this section.

> Source: Mansel, Phase 2 — End-User Interviews. Questions preserved verbatim.

### 1. Daily role & responsibilities
- Walk through a typical day or week in this role. What are the 1–3 most common tasks?
- How much of the day goes to the core job vs. administrative or repetitive tasks?

### 2. Step-by-step process deep dive
- Walk through the workflow you picked at a high level. Which part is most manual or takes longest?
- What information has to be gathered, where does it come from, and what gets created at the end?

### 3. Tools & frustrations
- What software is the doer in all day?
- Where is there double-entry, copy-paste, or tool frustration?

### 4. Pain points & wishlist
- How is work currently tracked and reported on?

### 5. Cultural & change management
- What would make the doer actually trust a new process or AI assist here?

### Add-ons — precision + adoption psychology
- **Quality bar.** How do you know you've done this task correctly? What does "done well" mean?
- **Time reallocation.** If the most repetitive part disappeared tomorrow, what higher-value work would replace it?
- **Open-loop check.** When you finish this task, who confirms it's done? Or does the work just disappear into someone else's inbox? *(Closed loops have confirmation. Open loops are where errors hide.)*

### 6A. Sales Discovery Deep Dive

> Source: Mansel, "Sales/Business Dev — Exec" playbook. ~90–120 min of writing if you do it thoroughly. Numbers matter — pull them from your CRM as you go.

**1. Current performance baseline.**
- What pressure are you under this quarter?
- What's your specific revenue target?
- Which KPI keeps you up at night — the one that if it fails is game over?
- Paint a picture of your sales org: team size, roles, and who owns prospecting, closing, renewals.
- Current win rate, average deal size, new opportunities per month, typical close cycle, and weekly team hours.
- If win rate, qualified pipeline, or cycle time improved slightly, what's that worth this quarter?

**2. Process & workflow mapping.**
- Last deal that took way longer than it should have — what happened?
- Typical sales process from lead to close.
- Where do deals get stuck or slow down?
- If lead volume doubled tomorrow, what would break first?
- Make-or-break moment in your sales process — the keystone that holds everything together.
- Which team-to-team handoffs create the most problems?

**3. Tools & data quality.**
- Official tools in the stack — CRM, email sequences, data enrichment, calling, proposals?
- Which tools do reps skip or work around, and where does bad data cost time or deals?
- Biggest data problems? (Wrong contacts, missing info, bad-fit leads, duplicates.) How often do they show up?

**4. Pain points & strategic priorities.**
- 3 biggest obstacles to hitting revenue target — rank them.
- Which team-to-team handoffs create the most rework / errors / delays?
- If these problems persist 90 more days, what happens to the business?
- Real cost — missed targets, lost deals, compliance issues?

**5. Opportunity prioritization.**
- Keystone of the entire sales op — if this one thing goes wrong, what gets irrelevant?
- Biggest opportunity right now — more qualified leads at the top, faster cycles, or higher close rates? Pick one and explain why.
- How would you know in 30 days you're succeeding?
- What moves the needle first: cycle time, win rate, or qualified pipeline volume?

**6. Change management reality check.**
- Who are your champions, resisters, and fence-sitters?
- A change that actually stuck — what made it work?
- What would have to be true to get 80% of reps using something new every week?

**7. Risk & compliance.**
- Biggest serious incident or near-miss in the last 6 months.
- Single biggest point of failure today.
- Recent incidents that cost money or deals.
- If the CRM went down for 48 hours, walk through what happens.

**8. Data access & next steps.**
- Reflect back: your #1 priority is _______. Right?
- Who controls the data needed to validate this?
- When can read-only access happen?

### 6B. End-User Workflow Deep Dive

> Source: Mansel, "End-User Workflow Interview Playbook." ~90–120 min of writing. Fill out as the person doing the work.

**1. Daily role & responsibilities.**
- Walk through yesterday — what was actually done vs. what was planned.
- 1–3 things done most often each day.
- Time on main job vs. admin overhead.
- How many times is the most common task done daily / weekly? What makes the busiest day hard?
- Workload patterns (seasonal, end-of-month, etc.).
- If the most repetitive task disappeared, what would replace it?

**2. Step-by-step process deep dive.**
- Last time [the workflow you picked] took forever — what went wrong?
- Walk through the exact steps when it goes normally.
- Which part takes the longest or feels most tedious?
- What information has to be gathered, and where does it come from?
- Where do you have to stop and think, make a judgment call, or ask someone what to do next?
- What makes this task easy some days and hard others?
- How does the process change for different deals / customers / situations?
- When do errors typically happen, and how are mistakes caught before they become a problem?
- How do you know you've done this correctly?
- What do you do with bad or incomplete information?
- Last time you had to wait for someone else to do your job.
- Who do you check with during this process?
- Where do you hand things off?
- Make-or-break step in the process.

**3. Tools & system friction.**
- Software you live in all day.
- Where do tools, double-entry, copy-paste, or unofficial shortcuts create friction?
- What information do you look up over and over?
- What do you do when the system doesn't work the way you need?
- Personal spreadsheets, notes, or trackers?

**4. Pain points & automation wishlist.**
- If you had an assistant for one day, what would they do?
- How do you track work and report progress?
- What reports, updates, or status notes do you create manually?
- What would make you 20% more productive starting next week?

**5. Change management & AI readiness.**
- Last time they changed how you do something at work — how did that go?
- What made it successful or a disaster?
- If AI could help with part of your job, what would be your first concern?
- What parts of your work would you *never* want automated? Why?
- How would you want to double-check that an AI did something right?
- How much time would you invest learning something new if it saved time later?

**6. Workflow observation setup.**
- Could you record yourself doing this task end-to-end? (Loom, screen recording.) Attach it to the audit if so.
- Personal cheat sheets, templates, or reference docs — attach them.

## Section 7 — Turn the workflow into Step Cards

You already described the workflow in plain English. Now break it into individual steps so an agency can estimate, quote, and build without re-interviewing you.

The previous Tally draft had a placeholder that said Annabel would replace this with a Google Sheets template URL. That simply meant: a spreadsheet template could make this section easier later. It is not required for launch. The form can collect Step Cards directly in a long-answer field, with an optional upload for anyone who already has a process doc, spreadsheet, screenshot, or Loom.

> Source: Mansel, "Map the Process & Find the Opportunities." Step Card preserved with two Bo additions (DRI, open-loop).

### List the steps

Number each step in the workflow, start to finish. Most workflows have 5–15 steps. If you have more than 20, you've probably bundled multiple workflows — pick one.

### Fill a Step Card for every step

| Field | What goes here |
|---|---|
| **Step #** | 1, 2, 3… |
| **Step name** | "Pull contact from CRM" / "Draft proposal" / "Send invoice" |
| **Owner / Role** | Who does it (job title, not name) |
| **Systems** | Tools used in this step |
| **Inputs → Outputs** | What comes in / what leaves |
| **Volume** | Per day or per week |
| **Doing Time (AHT)** | Minutes actually working |
| **Wait / Queue Time** | Minutes / hours waiting on others or systems |
| **Error / Rework %** | Rough estimate + common causes |
| **SLA** | Target time + breach rate, if tracked |
| **Data Sensitivity** | PII / Finance / HR / None |
| **Controls** | Approvals, dual control, exception rules |
| **DRI today** | Named role accountable for the *outcome* of this step. "None" is a valid answer — and a flag. |
| **Open or Closed loop** | Does someone confirm this step is done? Or does work just disappear into the next inbox? |

> If you can't fill these for every step, your map isn't audit-grade yet. Go back to your Section 5 / 6 answers and fill the gaps. The DRI and open-loop fields are Bo's additions — they're the two fields most clients skip and the two that predict whether an automation will hold up.

### Tag friction on each step

| Tag | Meaning | Apply when… |
|---|---|---|
| 🟡 **Time Sink** | Repeated manual effort | Doing time > 15 min per instance, OR the person is overqualified, OR repeated daily/weekly |
| 🟠 **Wait / Handoff** | Queue time > doing time | Wait > doing, OR requires someone else's sign-off, OR frequent delays |
| 🔴 **Quality Risk** | Errors / rework | Error rate > 10%, OR mistakes break downstream work, OR no QA / checklist |
| 🟣 **Compliance / Data Risk** | Sensitive data + weak controls | Step touches PII / Finance / HR / contracts AND has no clear controls |
| 🔁 **Open Loop** | No confirmation | No one confirms this step is done, no signed-off output |
| 🟢 / ⚪ / ⚫ | Adoption psychology | Champion present / Fearful / Skeptical gatekeeper |

> Most ROI is in 🟠 waits and 🔴 rework, not 🟡 manual tasks. And 🔁 open loops are where compounding errors hide — fix those before automating. (Mansel + Bo.)

## Section 8 — Find your keystone

A keystone is the step where if it fails, *nothing else matters*. Most workflows have one. Sometimes two. Almost never more.

> Source: Mansel, "Keystone Selection Process."

For each step (or the friction-tagged steps at minimum), ask the 5 questions:

1. **Which step is the keystone?** The one where failure collapses the workflow, loses the customer, or creates the costliest cleanup later.
2. **Why is that step the keystone?** Mention multiplier effect, point of no return, customer visibility, or downstream cleanup cost.

Mark your keystone(s) with ⭐ on the Step Cards. Keystones get a priority boost in Section 10.

## Section 9 — Run QDOAA on your tagged steps

> Source: Mansel, QDOAA verbatim. The single most important sequencing rule in the audit.

Use the Step Cards from the previous section. For each step you tagged with friction — especially your ⭐ keystone — run QDOAA before deciding what to automate.

| Letter | Prompt | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| **Q**uestion | What's the actual purpose? Who said this is necessary? What happens if we stop doing it? What are we assuming, and should we challenge it? | Identify bloated or legacy tasks. Cut the "shoulds." |
| **D**elete | What contributes no measurable value? What can be removed with zero or minimal negative impact? What feels good but isn't necessary? | Remove clutter before optimizing noise. |
| **O**ptimize | If you had half the resources, how would you do this? What's the 20% of actions creating 80% of results? | Sharpen ROI. |
| **A**ccelerate | Where can you move faster without breaking quality? What can run in parallel? | Apply force strategically. |
| **A**utomate | What can be delegated, templatized, automated? What SOPs save 10+ hours / month? | **Only now**, once Q-D-O-A have been done. |

> "Automating an efficient 3-step process is cheaper and more performant than automating a bloated 7-step mess. You get faster ROI because you're not paying for unnecessary automation." — Mansel

Most workflows lose 30–40% of their steps to Q-D-O alone, before automation enters the picture. If you're about to recommend automating a 12-step process, run this first — the answer might be a 7-step process that doesn't need automation at all.

For each friction-tagged Step Card, write 1–2 sentences answering each letter.

## Section 10 — Score your opportunities

Every tag is a candidate fix. Score each one and rank.

> Source: Mansel, "Opportunity Scoring Categories" + "Build the Op Matrix."

### Turn every tag into a candidate fix

- 🟡 Time Sink → automate data entry, AI-assisted drafting / summarization, auto-enrich, routing
- 🟠 Wait / Handoff → auto-notifications, SLA-based routing, parallelization, self-serve portals
- 🔴 Quality Risk → validation rules, AI QA, duplicate detection, playbooks, checklists
- 🟣 Compliance Risk → on-prem RAG, PII redaction, RBAC, logging, retention policies
- 🔁 Open Loop → confirmation hooks, closed-loop status callbacks, SLA timers with escalation *(Bo)*

### Score each candidate 1–5 on six dimensions

| Score | Impact | Effort *(lower = better)* | Risk *(lower = better)* | Adoption | Confidence | Data Readiness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **5** | Moves multiple KPIs hard | Huge (multi-team, months) | High — touches customers / money / compliance | Easy — champions exist, obvious painkiller | Observed baseline + test proof | Clean, accessible, permissioned |
| **4** | Strong on ≥2 KPIs | High but doable (weeks) | Medium-high — could cause customer impact | Some champions, little resistance | Observed data or clear precedent | Mostly clean, minor wrangling |
| **3** | Moderate (one KPI) | Medium | Medium — slows things if it fails | Mixed enthusiasm, training required | Precedent exists, not tested here | Exists but siloed / messy |
| **2** | Small or indirect | Low (simple SOP) | Low-medium | Hard — skeptical, adoption effort | Interviews only | Incomplete, permissions missing |
| **1** | No measurable impact | Trivial (one Zap) | Low — no consequence | Very hard — active resistance | Pure guesswork | None, blocked, wrong format |

### Calculate priority

```
Priority = (Impact × Confidence) − (Effort + Risk + (6 − Adoption) + (6 − Data Readiness))
```

**Keystones get +2 on Impact** before the calculation. Higher priority = do sooner.

### Plot on the 2×2

| | **Low effort** | **High effort** |
|---|---|---|
| **High impact** | **Quick Wins** — start here (target: 90 days) | **Big Swings** — sequence after Quick Wins |
| **Low impact** | Nice-to-have | Deprioritize |

> "Quick wins prove the model works before you ask for bigger budget. Builds trust. Generates cash / time savings that fund the big swings." — Mansel

## Section 11 — ROI + Cost of Inaction

Put dollars on every Quick Win. This is the part the agency will quote against. Skipping it means the agency proposes a fee with no anchor.

> Source: Mansel, "ROI Summary (The Money Slide)" + calculator rules.

### Fill the table

One row per Quick Win.

Optional upload: any spreadsheet or notes you already use to estimate savings, revenue impact, team hours, or ROI.

| Fix | Baseline hrs / wk | Time saved % | Hours saved / wk | Loaded $ / hr | Annual cost saved | Realloc % | Value / hr (rev) | Annual revenue uplift | **Annual total impact** | One-time cost | Annual run-rate cost | Payback (months) | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | | | | | | | | | Low / Med / High |

### Calculator rules

**A) Direct cost savings**
1. Hours saved / week = Baseline hours / week × % time saved
2. Annual cost saved = Hours saved / week × Loaded $ / hr × 52

> Loaded $ / hr = (salary + benefits + taxes + overhead) / 2080. Don't use bare salary.

**B) Revenue uplift (capacity redeployed)**
1. Revenue hours unlocked = Hours saved / week × Realloc %
2. Annual revenue uplift = Revenue hours unlocked × Value / hr × 52

> Value / hr: if 2 hours closes a $5,000 deal, value / hr = $2,500. Pull from your sales historicals.

**C) Net impact & payback**
- Annual total impact = Annual cost saved + Annual revenue uplift
- Annual net = Annual total impact − Annual run-rate cost
- Payback (months) = One-time cost ÷ (Annual net / 12)

**D) Confidence**
- **Low** — interviews / self-report only
- **Med** — observed + baselines
- **High** — pilot data exists

> Don't hand-wave. If you're guessing on Hours saved %, mark Confidence Low. The agency will sanity-check this in their scoping call.

## Section 12 — Quick Wins: what you'd build first

Pick **2–3** fixes from the top of your scored list. For each, fill the card below. This is what the agency will quote and build against.

> Source: Mansel (success thresholds) + Bo (internal owner, acceptance rules).

### Quick Win card (two required, third optional)

| Field | What goes here |
|---|---|
| **Fix name** | "AI-drafted outbound emails," "Auto-classify support tickets," "Auto-draft client weekly report" |
| **Step(s) it touches** | Step #s from your map |
| **Current → Future** | One sentence each. *Current: SDR writes each email from scratch, 8 min each. Future: AI drafts from CRM context, SDR edits, 90 sec each.* |
| **Internal owner** | Named role inside your business who owns the *outcome* of this fix. Not the agency — you. *(Bo. If no one owns it, the automation drifts in 60 days.)* |
| **Success criteria** | The specific numbers that would prove this fix worked, by when, and what result would mean it needs to be changed or stopped. *Example: "Email drafting time drops from 8 minutes to 90 seconds by week 4, quality stays at 95%+, and if adoption is under 30%, the workflow needs to change."* |
| **Acceptance rules** | 3–5 specific rules the agency's build must hit. *(Bo's test harness, in light form.)* Examples for AI-drafted outbound: "Must reference something specific from lead's website." "Tone must match 5 sample emails." "Subject under 60 chars." "No more than 2 questions per email." |
| **Annual total impact ($)** | From your ROI row |
| **One-time cost (est.)** | Rough — the agency will refine |

Quick Win #3 should be filled only if there is a third genuinely clear candidate. Two strong, agency-quoteable quick wins are better than three vague ones.
