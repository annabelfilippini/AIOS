# Find Your AI Operating System Starting Point

Working draft for AnnabelFilippini.com and the Agency Audit Network.

## Core Offer

Find Your AI Operating System Starting Point helps a person understand where AI belongs in their work or business, what should not be automated yet, and what kind of operating system they need around their workflows, tools, knowledge, and team.

The audit is not a prompt pack, tool list, or generic automation brainstorm. It is a practical diagnosis that turns business friction into an implementation-ready roadmap.

## One-Liner

I help founders, operators, and small business owners find their AI Operating System starting point, then turn it into a practical roadmap they can build themselves or hand to the right implementation partner.

## Best-Fit Clients

- Owner-led small businesses with repeatable work but messy systems.
- Local service businesses, ecommerce/product brands, agencies, consultants, and small teams.
- Businesses already using AI casually but without a shared system.
- Founders who feel trapped in lead follow-up, customer support, admin, reporting, content, or delivery work.
- Teams considering an AI agency but unsure what they actually need built.
- Warm referrals from Cooldown, Annabel's personal network, and life coach referrals.

## Not A Fit

- Companies looking for a magic chatbot without workflow change.
- Teams unwilling to share basic process, tool, or KPI context.
- Businesses expecting implementation outcomes before an agency scope is confirmed.
- Highly regulated use cases that require formal legal, security, or compliance review before discovery.

## Positioning

Most businesses try to add AI one tool at a time. That usually creates more noise: scattered prompts, unused automations, unclear data rules, and no way to know whether the work is actually improving.

The better move is to design an AI Operating System: the repeatable way the business uses AI across acquisition, delivery, support, and operations.

The audit finds the right starting point for the person taking it. If the founder takes it, the answer may be a personal AIOS that makes their own work easier first. If a team lead takes it, the answer may be a team AIOS around shared workflows. If the business is large enough, the answer may be a company-wide AIOS with policies, knowledge bases, automations, training, and implementation partners.

## AIOS Levels

### 1. Personal AIOS

For founders, operators, consultants, and solo owners.

Focus areas:
- research and decision support
- writing and content
- inbox, calendar, and follow-up
- notes and knowledge base
- personal task and project workflows
- reusable prompts, skills, and operating routines

Best when the main bottleneck is one person carrying too much context.

### 2. Team AIOS

For small teams with shared workflows.

Focus areas:
- lead intake and follow-up
- sales handoffs
- customer support triage
- delivery workflows
- shared SOPs and templates
- role-based AI assistants
- tool connections and automations

Best when the bottleneck is handoffs, repeated work, inconsistent execution, or work living in one person's head.

### 3. Company AIOS

For larger SMBs or multi-department teams.

Focus areas:
- approved AI tools and data rules
- company knowledge base
- department-level workflows
- prompt and SOP libraries
- automation roadmap
- human approval points
- training plan
- measurement dashboard
- maintenance and health checks

Best when the business needs AI usage to become shared infrastructure, not individual experimentation.

## Free Mini-Audit

Purpose: give the visitor a useful first diagnosis and route them toward the right next step.

Format: 7-10 questions on the website.

Primary CTA language: **Find Your AI Operating System Starting Point**.

The free audit should identify:
- which AIOS level they likely need: personal, team, company, or cleanup first
- which pod has the strongest opportunity: acquisition, delivery, support, or operations
- what kind of friction is most visible: time sink, wait/handoff, quality risk, data/compliance risk
- whether they seem ready for implementation or need process cleanup first

Output:
- one short diagnosis
- 2-3 likely opportunity areas
- one caution or prerequisite
- CTA to book the paid audit

The free audit should not produce a full roadmap, vendor recommendations, ROI math, or agency match. Those belong in the paid audit.

## Paid Audit

Working name: AIOS Starting Point Audit.

Purpose: produce a clear, implementation-ready diagnosis for the person taking the audit. The recommendation may be personal, team, or company-wide depending on their role, business size, and bottlenecks.

Recommended format:
- intake form
- 60-90 minute founder/operator interview
- optional lightweight website/tool review
- optional follow-up clarification by email
- 5-7 page report
- 30-minute review call
- agency match recommendation included in the early version when implementation support makes sense

## Paid Audit Deliverables

### 1. Business Snapshot

- what the business sells
- who it serves
- how it gets customers
- team size and roles
- current tools
- current AI usage
- main bottlenecks
- stated goals

### 2. AIOS Level Recommendation

Recommend one of:
- Personal AIOS
- Team AIOS
- Company AIOS
- Cleanup before automation

Explain why in plain language.

### 3. Four-Pod Workflow Scan

Use the pods:
- Acquisition: how customers are found, nurtured, and converted
- Delivery: how the product or service is fulfilled
- Support: how customers are helped after purchase
- Operations: how the back office runs

For each pod, capture:
- visible workflows
- repeated work
- handoffs
- tool friction
- data or knowledge gaps
- candidate AIOS components

### 4. Friction Diagnosis

Tag the most important friction:
- Time Sink: repeated manual effort
- Wait/Handoff: work stalls between people or systems
- Quality Risk: errors, rework, inconsistent output
- Data/Compliance Risk: sensitive data, weak controls, unclear permissions

Use this to avoid automating the wrong thing.

### 5. Keystone Finding

Identify the keystone step: the workflow moment that has the highest leverage or highest downside if it fails.

Examples:
- first lead response
- quote/proposal creation
- onboarding handoff
- customer support triage
- order fulfillment update
- CRM data capture
- content production bottleneck

The keystone should guide the first implementation priority.

### 6. Opportunity Matrix

Score the top opportunities using:
- impact
- effort
- risk
- adoption
- confidence
- data readiness

Keep the client-facing version simple. The goal is clarity, not spreadsheet theater.

### 7. 90-Day Roadmap

Recommend 2-3 quick wins.

Each quick win should include:
- what changes
- why it matters
- owner
- implementation difficulty
- success metric
- guardrail
- kill/pivot condition

### 8. Agency Match Brief

If implementation support makes sense, include a handoff brief for agencies:
- business context
- selected quick wins
- required tools and data
- likely integrations
- risk/guardrail notes
- budget and timeline assumptions
- skills needed from the agency
- what should not be built yet

This is the bridge between Annabel's audit and a partner agency's build scope.

## Trust Principles

- Do not promise implementation results before partner scope is agreed.
- Disclose referral fees where appropriate.
- Use client-friendly language, not builder jargon.
- Lead with business outcomes, then explain systems.
- Treat AIOS as a design concept, not a proprietary claim.
- Give credit internally where frameworks were inspired by Mansel Scheffel's AIOS model and related audit materials.
- Keep client-facing material specific, non-hypey, and beautiful.
