## 3. Recommended audit upgrade — the "Annabel Audit v3"

A single audit that absorbs the best of both and works for Dad's employees today + product-brand clients later.

### Structure

**Page 1 — Plain-English summary (Bo).** Three sentences max. What we found, what to do first, what it's worth. Owner can show their leadership.

**Page 2 — The Six-Layer Business Brain Map (Bo).** For each layer, score 0–5 on how complete + how accessible it is today. Visualize as a radar chart. *This is the audit's diagnostic centerpiece.*

| Layer | What we look for | Common gaps |
|-------|------------------|-------------|
| 1. Identity | Mission, products, ICP, brand voice, founder POV — written down somewhere stable | Lives in founder's head, never written |
| 2. Critical context | Current quarter goals, active campaigns, current pricing, deals in flight | Last month's numbers in a Slack thread |
| 3. Working memory | This week's sprint, in-progress deals, this week's content | Mental notes, untracked |
| 4. Episodic memory | *Why* decisions were made (paused outbound, fired vendor, pivoted segment) | Walks out the door with employees |
| 5. Long-term knowledge | Playbooks, won/lost deals, case studies, customer interviews | Scattered across Drives |
| 6. Background decay | Process that promotes frequently-cited info and decays stale info | Almost never exists |

**Page 3 — Department / Pod Audit (Mansel pods, Bo departments).** Four columns: Acquisition / Delivery / Support / Operations. For each, list current processes + score:
- Open or closed loop? *(Bo)*
- Highest frequency × duration = impact score *(Bo's process map methodology)*
- Currently has a DRI? *(Bo)*
- Automatable / needs human review / must be human *(Bo)*

**Page 4 — Test Harness gaps (Bo).** For the top 3 processes flagged as automatable, draft the test harness rules. *Example for outbound:* "Email must reference something specific from the lead's website. Tone must match the rep's voice. Subject under 60 chars." This makes the audit *executable* — the partner who picks it up has a starting spec.

**Page 5 — 90-day priority roadmap (Bo's Phase 1 audit doc layer).** Three sprints of 30 days each. Each sprint = one department, top 2 processes wired.

**Page 6 — Implementation referral (Annabel's ATOM role).** Two-paragraph match: "Based on your audit, you fit Profile X. Recommended partner: Y. Their pricing tier: Z. Their delivery model: short description."

### The "old rule / new rule" table (Bo)

Lift Bo's table from the foundation video into every Deep Audit, customized per client. Pre-fill the old/new pairs; ask the owner which rules they're still operating by. This becomes the *qualitative* counterpart to the quantitative process-map score.

## 5. Specific application: Dad's employees

Constraint: marketing + sales roles in a small business context. Probably 1–4 employees per audit, mid-experience.

### Audit-on-an-employee shape

Two ways to scope the audit:
- **Audit the person** — what they personally execute, where their workflows leak, where AI could give them leverage. (One-Person AI Transformation framing.)
- **Audit the role** — what the role is supposed to produce, and whether the current execution matches.

**Recommendation: audit the role first, then the person.** The role audit identifies the gaps; the person audit identifies what to keep human.

### Minimum viable v1 for Dad's employees

1. Pre-call: 5-question intake (Bo style — async questionnaire)
2. 60-min discovery call: walk through their typical week, identify open loops, identify domain knowledge that lives only in their head
3. Output: 2-page Quick Read with:
   - Top 3 processes that should run as closed loops
   - Top 1 thing to capture into the business brain *this week* (probably episodic memory — why decisions were made)
   - One starter skill they could install on Day 1 (the daily brief is Bo's recommended wedge)

### The starter skill bet: Daily Brief

Both Mansel ("Daily Brief" exists as a blueprint) and Bo ("first automation that proves the framework works") recommend the same first install. *This is the wedge for every Dad's-employee engagement.* Build it once with the right shape; reuse across employees.

What the daily brief produces:
- Yesterday's calls / messages / customer interactions summarized
- Where employee stands on quarterly goal
- 3 priorities for today
- One "you might have forgotten" reference to a past decision (this is the episodic-memory hook)

Mansel has a Daily Brief blueprint zip in his Blueprint Library. Worth requesting access to or recreating in Annabel's own version.
