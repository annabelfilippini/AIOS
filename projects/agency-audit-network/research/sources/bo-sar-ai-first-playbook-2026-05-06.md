## The seven concepts in order

### 1. AI as operating system, not tool

YC framing: "AI should not be a tool your company just uses. It should be the operating system your company runs on."

Tool vs. methodology distinction:
- **Tool** — ChatGPT in a browser tab, a chatbot, a Zapier flow. Plateaus.
- **Methodology** — Every decision in the business runs through one question: "Why can't AI do this?" Restructures how the business operates. Compounds.

> "6 months from now, the AI running your business will know more about your operations than most employees. Every sales call, every client interaction, every process — it all feeds back into the system and makes it smarter."

### 2. Closed loops vs. open loops

Borrowed from control systems engineering.

- **Open loop** — Make decision → execute → maybe check results. No systematic feedback. Most businesses run this way.
- **Closed loop** — Thermostat. Continuously monitors output, adjusts process to meet goal.

Example closed-loop sales process:
- Every call transcribed automatically
- AI analyzes objections, what worked, what didn't
- Analysis feeds into next call prep
- Follow-up emails written based on what was discussed (not template)
- CRM auto-updated
- System learns best patterns over time

YC quote: "With self-improving agents, your company should run as a closed loop."

Multiply across every department: marketing, sales, delivery, operations, finance.

### 3. The "company brain" / "business brain"

YC's framing (from a separate YC clip Bo plays): "The biggest blocker to AI automation of companies is no longer the models. The real blocker is the domain knowledge inside companies. Every business has critical know-how scattered everywhere — in people's heads, email threads, Slack, support tickets, databases. Companies only work because humans vaguely remember where that knowledge is."

The brain is "a living map of how a company works — how refunds get handled, how pricing exceptions are decided, how engineers respond to technical incidents." Then AI agents use skills files to actually do the work safely and consistently.

YC quote: "Every company in the world is going to need one of these."

In a typical company:
- Pricing logic → spreadsheets or founder's head
- Onboarding processes → Google Docs
- Sales templates → individual inboxes
- Brand guidelines → wherever
- Process documentation → wherever
- Client history → scattered

The wire step in the framework = building this brain.

### 4. Software factories + test harnesses

Borrowed from how Jack Dorsey runs his AI team at Block.

**Software factory model:**
- Human writes specification (what to build)
- Human writes tests (what success looks like)
- AI agents generate output and iterate until tests pass
- Human defines what to build + judges output. AI does the building.

**Test harness = set of criteria defining "good enough."** A checklist the AI runs its own output against before showing you.

Example test harness for a proposal-writing skill:
- Must include client company name and industry
- Pricing within standard range
- References at least one relevant case study
- Under 3 pages
- Conversational tone

AI writes → self-checks → revises → shows polished output, not a first draft.

> "This is how you go from being the person who does the work to the person who defines what good work looks like and reviews the output."

Apply test harnesses everywhere: email campaigns, content pieces, sales follow-ups, reports.

### 5. The new org chart (Jack Dorsey's three roles)

Why middle managers existed: human middleware routing information up and down. If the company is queryable by AI, you need almost no human middleware.

Dorsey quote (Bo's paraphrase): "If you keep the same org chart and management structure, you've missed the shift entirely. The company has to be rebuilt with humans at the edge just guiding it, not routing information through it."

Three roles every company will have:

1. **IC — Individual Contributor** — Builder-operator. Not limited to engineers anymore. In an AI-first company, *everyone* builds. Sales builds, ops builds, support builds. "Everyone comes to meetings with working prototypes, not pitch decks."

2. **DRI — Directly Responsible Individual** — Not a classic manager. One person, one outcome. Focuses on strategy + customer results, not on managing people. "DRI for revenue growth," "DRI for client satisfaction." Outcome, not process.

3. **AI Founder** — Still builds, leads by example. *You cannot outsource your understanding of these tools and systems.* "You cannot outsource your conviction on the power of these tools." If you don't understand what's possible, you'll build something mediocre.

### 6. Token maxing

YC's phrase. Old way: scale by adding headcount. New way: maximize token usage, not headcount.

YC advice: "You should be willing to run an uncomfortably high API bill because it's replacing what would have taken a far more expensive and inflated headcount."

Bo's math: $500/mo on AI tools + API costs replaces $10K–$20K/mo in human salaries for: content creation, lead research, proposal writing, data analysis, email campaigns.

> "It used to be a flex to have a 100-person team. That metric is becoming obsolete. The best companies going forward will not be the ones with the most employees — they will be the ones with the highest output per person."

### 7. Startups + small businesses have a structural advantage

YC's argument: large companies have legacy systems, thousands of people to retrain, established processes people are afraid to change. "Cruise ship trying to U-turn."

Small teams / solo operations: "speedboat." Can redesign from scratch today.

YC: "You can design your systems, workflows, and culture around AI from the start, and as a result, operate a thousand times faster than the incumbents."

Bo: 95%+ of businesses haven't adopted AI this way yet. "That gap is your opportunity."

## The 4-step framework (Learn → Wire → Automate → Scale)

| Step | Goal | Milestone |
|------|------|-----------|
| 1. **Learn** | Get comfortable with the tool (Claude Code or Claude Co-work). 1–2 weeks daily use. Build simple things. Have the "aha moment" where you realize what it can do. | You can open Claude Code and have a productive session without feeling lost. |
| 2. **Wire** | Make the business queryable + legible to AI. Structure CLAUDE.md hierarchy + Obsidian/Notion knowledge base. Connect live data sources (sales transcripts, Slack, CRM, Stripe). | Claude answers questions about your business without you re-explaining anything. |
| 3. **Automate** | Build AI departments (marketing/sales/delivery/ops). Each one runs as a closed loop. For every skill, define test harness; AI iterates until it meets the standard. Build daily morning brief first. | At least one department fully running with multiple skills + 1–2 chained workflows. |
| 4. **Scale** | Use freed bandwidth: more clients, more content, new offers. Every new initiative = new department, agents, skills. | Repeatable framework. Output multiplies without headcount. |

Sequential — can't skip steps.

## Cloud Code vs. Cloud Co-work (Bo's framing)

| | Claude Code | Claude Co-work |
|---|-------------|----------------|
| Form factor | CLI inside IDE (Cursor/VS Code) | Desktop app, no terminal |
| Power | Maximum: builds apps, runs scripts, any API, multi-step workflows | Wrapped, more user-friendly |
| Audience | Founders, solopreneurs, executives, small teams where each person owns their domain | Teams where owner needs to distribute access, manage permissions, control what each person can do |
| Sharing | Personal | Plug-in marketplace, can publish private packs, control which plug-ins are auto-installed, available, or required |

Both can run the same business brain + skills + workflows. Difference is interface and control.

## Old rule / New rule list (Bo's "what changes when you go AI-first")

| Old | New |
|-----|-----|
| Come to meetings with ideas/proposals | Come to meetings with working prototypes |
| Hire people to execute processes | Build AI skills to execute. Hire people to manage + improve AI departments |
| Scale by adding headcount | Scale by adding AI departments + skills. Token-max, not people-max |
| Business knowledge lives in heads, Confluence, scattered drives | Business knowledge lives in the business brain, structured + queryable |
| Review and approve everything manually | Define test harnesses. AI self-checks. You review output, not process |
| Middle managers route information | Intelligence layer routes information. Humans focus on outcomes |
| Speed limited by team availability | Speed limited only by how many skills you build |

## Direct lifts for Annabel's audit

1. **Open vs. closed loop is a clean diagnostic frame.** For each of Dad's employees' processes, ask: "Is this open or closed loop?" If open, why? What's the feedback mechanism that's missing? This is a single-question audit gate that's easier than asking "what should be automated."
2. **Test harness is a deliverable, not a concept.** For any skill/agent Annabel recommends, the audit should include the test harness rules ("a good outbound email must include X, Y, Z"). That's what makes the audit *executable* rather than abstract.
3. **The "old rule / new rule" table is a ready-made section for the Deep Audit PDF.** Customize per client: which old rules are they still operating by? Which new rules would unlock the biggest gain?
4. **DRI assignment is the org-chart half of the audit.** Process map says what to automate; DRI map says who owns the outcome once it's automated. Bo's framework doesn't explicitly include this in the audit doc — Annabel could.
5. **Bo's "AI Founder" principle is the buyer-side gating question.** If Dad's employees can't (or won't) develop their own conviction, the implementation will be mediocre. The audit should test for this and flag clients who need to be educated first.
