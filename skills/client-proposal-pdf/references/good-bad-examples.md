# Good And Bad Examples

## Good

- Vital Health proposal used a live review link, Phase 1 website summary, Phase
  2 backend setup, honest hour ranges, a running total, optional Instagram
  automation, terms, and next steps.
- It separated the $700 website from hourly backend coordination at $95/hr.
- It named Webflow hosting as an ongoing cost paid by the client.
- It kept the backend estimate flexible until the manager meeting clarified
  Cerbo, store, checkout, staff, and provider workflow.
- Final PDF was compressed to two clean pages instead of leaving the signature
  orphaned on a third page.

## Bad

- Hiding uncertainty by pretending backend setup is fully known before discovery.
- Bundling optional Instagram work into the core website phase.
- Using phrases Annabel already cut, such as "the approved", "no surprises", or
  long parentheticals that make the estimate feel defensive.
- Leaving dashes in title, phase headers, table ranges, or proposal body after
  Annabel asked for no dashes.
- Sending a PDF whose math does not match the table.

## Reusable Vital Health Discovery Questions

- Which system handles patient/client records, notes, billing, and portal access?
- Are products or services sold through that system, a third-party store, or a
  separate checkout?
- Where does a staff recommendation get added?
- Who fulfills orders?
- Are products public, portal-only, or both?
- Where do promotions happen: public site, checkout, backend coupon, or
  patient-specific discount?
